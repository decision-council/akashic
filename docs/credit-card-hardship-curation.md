## Verification and Freshness Boundaries

The schema and offline validator require explicit unknowns, source-backed documented facts, six distinct statuses, separate identity roles, and review dates. Tests cover attempts to upgrade missing evidence to unavailability, secondary reports masquerading as policy, wrong product scope, unavailable sources, missing references, stale/conflicting evidence, and accidental undeclared account fields. These checks do not determine whether prose is true; source and privacy review remain necessary.

The reader guide displays a dated snapshot and instructs readers to recheck. The CLI computes effective status using the supplied/current date and flags without rewriting the historical observation. A scheduled review date is a maintenance commitment, not a guarantee that a lender's policy will stay stable until then. No live crawler or automatic renewal of verification dates is introduced.

## Medium Article Candidate

**Title:** “No Published Hardship Program Doesn’t Always Mean No Help Exists.”

**Reader promise:** Learn what a source actually establishes, identify the right institution, and ask for terms you can evaluate before enrollment.

**Outline:**

1. Start with a fictional reader who cannot find a hardship page. Missing documentation leaves a question open.
2. Separate the brand, network, issuer, and service-provider roles; identify the actual product and current contact route.
3. Explain the six evidence states and the difference between dated public information and an account-specific offer.
4. Demonstrate explicit unknowns: an unpublished APR, reporting term, or duration is not a favorable default.
5. Use the short support questions to clarify assistance, eligibility while current, payment, fees/APR, account access, reporting, arrears, duration, and exit.
6. Close with written terms, a private confirmation record, follow-up, and the maintained guide.

Reverify any named example before publication. Avoid promises of approval, reduced APR, fees waived, preserved card access, or credit-score outcomes. Use invented circumstances only; do not adapt private customer conversations. Distinguish hardship arrangements from ordinary purchase installments, general APR negotiation, and debt settlement.
