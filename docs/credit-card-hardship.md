# Credit-card hardship: verify the product, then ask for the terms

**Start with the issuer and contact route on your current statement or card.** Ask whether payment assistance is available for your exact product and account. A missing web page does not establish that help is unavailable, and a published program does not establish that you qualify.

This U.S. starter registry records public evidence, not customer accounts or approvals. Sources were checked **September 18, 2026**; the next scheduled review is **October 18, 2026**, or sooner if the issuer, contact route, or policy changes. Recheck before relying on a record. Return to [Financial Relief](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#debt-and-payment-hardship) for broader assistance and counseling routes.

## 1. Identify the account without publishing it

Privately check the product name, legal issuer, current servicer, country, and the agreement applicable to the account. The logo, payment network, app, issuer, and company answering support may have different roles. An issuer-wide program does not automatically apply to every partner-issued card using the same network.

Use the contact on your statement, authenticated account, or verified official site. Avoid sending identifiers or documents to a number or address copied from an unverified search result. Keep account numbers, balances, hardship circumstances, statements, and support transcripts outside public issues and this registry.

## 2. Read the evidence state and its date

| State | What the evidence supports | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `published-program` | Current first-party material describes a defined program within the recorded scope. | Ask whether your exact account qualifies and obtain its written offer. |
| `published-contact-path` | A first-party support/assistance route is documented; this record does not establish complete program terms. | Ask who handles hardship and which terms apply. It is not a finding that no other public program page exists. |
| `case-by-case-reported` | An attributed public report describes individual review; it is not established general policy. | Confirm directly. Another person's offer does not establish your terms. |
| `no-public-evidence-found` | A documented, bounded review found no program evidence in the inspected sources. | Ask directly; absence of evidence does not prove unavailability. |
| `confirmed-unavailable` | An issuer/servicer explicitly states unavailability for the exact product and recorded scope. | Check the date and limits; do not generalize one account's denial to every customer or future request. |
| `unknown` | Evidence is insufficient, overdue for review, inaccessible, conflicting, or no longer matches the issuer/product. | Reverify before making a policy claim. |

**Unknown terms stay unknown.** An empty APR field is not zero interest; missing credit-reporting language is not a promise of no impact. An inaccessible page does not prove a program ended. A general article about financial hardship does not prove its publisher offers a program.

## 4. Ask a short set of questions

The [CFPB recommends contacting the card company promptly](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-i-cant-pay-my-credit-card-bills-en-1697/) when the minimum is unaffordable, explaining the situation, affordable amount, requested duration, and when normal payments might resume. Be factual about what you can afford; do not promise a payment to obtain an offer you cannot sustain.

**Opening question:** “Do you offer hardship or payment assistance for this specific product and account, including while it is still current? Which team can review it, and can you send the exact proposed terms in writing before I enroll?”

Then verify:

- **Payment and arrears:** required amount, first due date, frequency, and what happens to existing past-due amounts.
- **APR and fees:** whether interest changes, which fees are waived or deferred, and when ordinary terms resume.
- **Card access:** whether purchases, the limit, rewards, or the account itself are restricted, frozen, or closed; what restores access.
- **Credit reporting:** how enrollment, prior delinquency, and payments during the plan are reported; whether an existing late mark remains.
- **Duration and exit:** length, renewal/re-enrollment limits, early exit, missed-payment consequences, and what terminates relief.
- **Evidence and decision:** documents needed, secure submission route, response timing, and what remains due while the request is pending.

If support says no, ask whether that means no program for the product, no eligibility for this account at present, no approval for the requested terms, or the wrong servicing team. Record the actual answer and its scope. Do not convert an agent's account-specific response into permanent public policy.

## 5. Confirm, document, and follow through

Before enrollment, compare the written offer with your essential expenses and realistic payment capacity. Confirm the start date, treatment of any scheduled payments, and how to verify that the arrangement took effect. A request, uploaded document, or support conversation does not itself change the payment obligation or pause reporting and collections.

Approval does not automatically remove accurate earlier reporting or establish how another issuer will handle a request.
