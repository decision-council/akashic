# Compare education pathways and verify credentials

**U.S. first edition.** Start with the [short guide and verification resources](../lists/work-and-learning/README.md#education-pathways). Rules differ by state, institution, program, and intended occupation. For study or work elsewhere, ask that destination's education authority and credential recipient. This worksheet helps organize questions; it does not decide admission, funding, credit, licensure, or immigration status.

## Choose a route

| Your goal | A useful first conversation |
| --- | --- |
| Reading, writing, mathematics, or English skills | Ask an adult-education provider about assessment, placement, class format, eligibility, and language support. CareerOneStop's Find Training menu includes Adult Basic Education; an American Job Center can help locate classes. |
| High school equivalency | Use CareerOneStop's state equivalency route, then confirm the accepted exam, age/residency rules, accommodations, fees, and official testing arrangements with the state program. A preparation course is separate from the credential. |
| Community college or a degree | Ask an admissions/transfer adviser whether the specific program supports your goal, which prerequisites apply, and whether part-time or online study changes the sequence. Confirm any intended transfer with the receiving college. |
| Vocational training or professional certification | Compare the specific award with actual job requirements using the [career and apprenticeship guide](../lists/work-and-learning/README.md#start-here). Ask the relevant licensing board whether this program meets its educational requirements. |
| Existing learning or qualifications | Ask the college about transfer, exams, portfolios, military/workplace learning, and credit-for-prior-learning assessment. For an overseas qualification, start with the recipient's evaluation instructions. |

An **educational certificate** records a course/program award; **professional certification** attests to criteria set by a certifying body; an **occupational license** is government permission for regulated work; a **degree** is an academic award. Verify institutional and, where relevant, program accreditation. Similar names do not establish equivalent recognition.

## Compare, verify, check cost, apply, confirm

### 1. Compare two realistic options

Use College Scorecard for institution and field-of-study context. Read each measure's definition, date, and population; missing data is not zero, and a college average is not a forecast for you. Compare the actual campus, delivery format, qualification level, completion requirements, and schedule. Ask about required placements, equipment, travel, and support when a course becomes unavailable.

Free learning libraries remain in [Awesome Abundance Education](../lists/awesome-abundance/education-and-learning/README.md). Learning a skill there does not automatically earn recognized academic credit.

### 2. Verify the institution, program, and permission to operate

Use DAPIP to check the exact institution/campus and any required program accreditation, then confirm the current status and scope with the listed accrediting agency. ED recognizes accreditors; it does not itself accredit colleges. DAPIP is limited to its reported U.S. postsecondary coverage. An absent record alone does not prove fraud.

Ask admissions which state agency authorizes the school and independently check with that agency. For distance study, ask about authorization where you will be located. Separately use CareerOneStop's License Finder to locate the responsible occupational regulator; confirm current education, exam, practice, and application requirements directly. School authorization, accreditation, and your professional license are separate checks.

### 3. Check the full cost and actual aid

Ask the aid office for the program's cost of attendance and a written offer for your enrollment pattern. Include tuition/fees, materials, living expenses, transport, and necessary technology. Compare the same time period and expected time to completion. Separate grants/scholarships, borrowing, work earnings, and your own funds; check conditions, renewal, timing, and any remaining gap. A calculator estimate or submitted FAFSA is not an award.

Start with the existing [Federal Student Aid, workforce, and public-program routes](../lists/awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#education-training-and-employment). FSA's [aid-offer comparison guidance](https://studentaid.gov/complete-aid-process/comparing-aid-offers) explains cost and debt comparison. Ask the administering agency about your program and personal eligibility. For Massachusetts unemployment training, use the [TOP guide](massachusetts-top-training.md): course approval, claimant approval, and tuition assistance are different decisions. Use [Financial Relief](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#student-loans) for existing student-loan problems.

### 4. Apply through verified channels

Confirm admission and aid deadlines separately, the required records, secure submission method, and receipt. Ask about [learner accommodations](../lists/awesome-abundance/README.md#disability-access-and-independent-living), accessible documents, language support, and a phone or in-person route if needed. Read cancellation/refund terms before paying; pause for unexplained enrollment pressure or guaranteed-job claims.

Keep application numbers, transcripts, financial information, identity records, and actual correspondence in private storage. Never put them in public issues or example worksheets. If an office cannot answer, ask who owns the question and agree on a follow-up date. For unresolved rights or access issues, use [education-rights resources](../lists/legal-help-and-law/README.md#education-and-student-rights); preserve actual notice deadlines while seeking help.

### 5. Confirm recognition before committing

For transfer, ask the **receiving institution** for its written evaluation: which credits apply to the intended major or only as electives, and which graduation requirements remain? Check grade, age, residency, and assessment rules; a transfer partnership's name alone is insufficient.

ACE recommendations help document evaluated prior learning. Match the course/exam version and recommendation dates, then ask the college how to request assessment. An ACE listing is not accreditation or a credit award. Ask about assessment fees and accepted evidence before ordering services; [ACE's learner guidance](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/Pages/Seeking-Credit.aspx) explains the receiving college's role.

For foreign qualifications, ask the receiving school, employer, or licensing board which evaluator, report, translation, and records it accepts **before paying**. ED does not perform evaluations or endorse an evaluation service. Acceptance for one purpose does not guarantee acceptance for another. This edition does not cover visas or permission to work.
