# Leaving COBRA without losing coverage in Massachusetts

For Massachusetts residents comparing coverage after employer insurance ends. Return to [Financial Relief and Assistance](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#massachusetts-health-coverage-transition) for the short orientation and [official resources](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#massachusetts-health-coverage-resources).

**Start here: apply before canceling current coverage.** A quote, eligibility result, or selected plan alone does not establish active insurance. Keep COBRA's existing payment and notice deadlines in view while arranging replacement coverage.

Sources checked **2026-09-17**; see the [dated source map and limitations](financial-relief-curation.md#massachusetts-coverage-source-map). This is public education, not an individual eligibility decision. Use the current coverage year, application instructions, plan documents, and written determinations. Free application help is available through the existing [MassHealth Application Help entry](../atlas/places/us-ma.md#benefits-food-and-housing), including when forms, language, or technology are a barrier.

## One next action

Open the step you need. Complete that action, keep its evidence privately, and return for the next step.

<details>
<summary>1. Apply before canceling current coverage</summary>

**Next action: start the official Massachusetts coverage application.** Use the [MassHealth application guide](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-masshealth-the-health-safety-net-or-the-childrens-medical-security-plan) or the existing [Massachusetts Health Connector gateway](../lists/public-services-and-support/README.md#health-care-and-medication). The application guide identifies who uses this route and directs seniors and people needing long-term care to the appropriate application.

Gather current coverage/end-date notices, income records, household/tax information, and information about insurance available through employment. Ask about acceptable alternatives for missing documents. Use the [income and household notes](#income-and-household) below; answer for the actual coverage year and preserve the submission receipt.

Ask the Connector which enrollment window applies and its exact deadline. Loss of job coverage can open special enrollment; the current Massachusetts [special-enrollment guide](https://betterhealthconnector.com/get-started/special-enrollment-period) generally allows 60 days following a qualifying event. MassHealth applications are available year-round; first-time or newly eligible ConnectorCare applicants may have a year-round route. These rules do not mean every person can switch any plan at any time.

COBRA exhaustion or loss of an employer contribution can create a switching opportunity. **Voluntary cancellation or nonpayment alone generally does not.** Verify the actual event and window using the existing [COBRA references](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#cobra-continuation-coverage); keep current coverage in force while resolving timing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Read the eligibility result</summary>

**Next action: read and save the written determination for each applicant.** Identify the program, assistance amount if any, requested evidence, response/appeal deadline, and next enrollment task. Family members can receive different results. A pending application is not approval.

Do not assume unemployment means MassHealth or ConnectorCare eligibility. Resolve missing information through the agency or an enrollment assister. If you disagree, follow the notice's review or appeal instructions without waiting past its deadline for a callback.

For MassHealth, follow the decision's coverage and health-plan selection instructions. For a Connector result, review the actual plans and approved assistance available to that applicant. Ask how existing COBRA or access to a new employer plan affects assistance; do not assume all coverage can receive subsidies simultaneously.

</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Compare plans and verify essential care</summary>

**Next action: complete the comparison worksheet for the strongest available options.** Use the exact plan and coverage year, its Summary of Benefits and Coverage, formulary, and network documents. Compare remaining COBRA continuity and deductible progress with replacement costs; neither option is automatically better.

Before selecting, work through the [provider and prescription checklist](#verify-providers-and-prescriptions). Treat an unanswered essential-care question as unresolved. Ask the insurer, provider, and prescribing team about transition arrangements before depending on them.

</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Enroll, satisfy the first-payment requirement, and confirm the start date</summary>

**Next action: complete enrollment and any required first premium, then obtain confirmation of the effective date.** The Connector's current [payment instructions](https://betterhealthconnector.com/how-to-pay) say the first full premium is due by the **23rd of the month before coverage starts**. Recheck the current deadline and payment receipt rules; missing the cutoff can move the earliest start date. Allow processing time.

For a zero-premium plan, complete enrollment and confirm that no payment is due. For MassHealth, follow its approval, effective-date, and any health-plan enrollment instructions; do not apply the Connector premium rule to every MassHealth member.

Save the selected plan and payment receipt, then confirm with the Connector or MassHealth and the insurer as applicable: **enrollment accepted, any required payment satisfied, exact start date, and coverage status for each person**. Ask how to verify benefits for care on that date if the ID card has not arrived. A payment receipt alone is not proof that coverage is active.

If records disagree or a payment is pending, resolve the discrepancy before requesting COBRA termination. Ask what coverage remains available during any delay.

</details>

<details>
<summary>5. Request COBRA termination after replacement is confirmed</summary>

**Next action: contact the COBRA administrator through its verified channel.** Proceed only after replacement enrollment, payment requirements, and the effective date are confirmed. Request a **last covered day immediately before the new start date**, and ask the administrator to confirm how its termination-date terminology works.

Check advance-notice requirements, permitted termination dates, premium obligations, dependent rights, and medical/dental/vision elections separately. If the administrator cannot align dates as requested, resolve the overlap or gap before approving the change. Do not assume retroactive cancellation, a prorated premium, or a refund.

Ask for written confirmation of the final covered day, affected people and benefits, and final balance. A submitted request is not a termination confirmation. Handle automatic payments only after the administrator confirms what remains owed and when coverage ends.

**Synthetic date example:** if replacement coverage is confirmed effective November 1, request October 31 as COBRA's last covered day. This illustrates the date relationship, not a promise that a particular administrator allows that termination date.

</details>

<details>
<summary>6. Save and reconcile both confirmations</summary>

**Next action: put the replacement and COBRA confirmations side by side.** Check the covered people, benefits, new start date, and old last covered day. Resolve discrepancies promptly with the responsible agency, carrier, or administrator.

Keep one private folder with the application receipt, income-estimate assumptions, eligibility determination, selected plan documents, first-payment receipt or no-payment confirmation, enrollment/effective-date confirmation, provider/drug verification notes, COBRA cancellation request and delivery proof, termination confirmation, and final billing record. Note who answered each unresolved question and when to follow up.

This public guide contains no personal case data. Keep employer names, member IDs, addresses, diagnoses, medication names, personal income, and private portal screenshots out of public issues and shared copies of the worksheet. Provide required information only through verified private channels.

</details>

<details>
<summary>7. Update the application when circumstances change</summary>

**Next action: report a material change through the appropriate agency account or contact route.** Examples include wages starting or ending, unemployment compensation changing, household/tax-filing changes, a move, or new employer coverage becoming available. Report the offered coverage and its start date, not only whether you accepted it.

Use the Connector's [income-update instructions](https://betterhealthconnector.com/help-center-answers/individuals-and-families/how-to-update-your-income-information) promptly and follow current notices. MassHealth says to [report changes](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/report-changes-to-masshealth) as soon as possible and **no later than 10 days after the change**. Confirm which agency needs the update; do not assume an employer's report updates the application.

Save the submission and read the new determination. A change may alter assistance or move someone between MassHealth, ConnectorCare, and ordinary marketplace coverage. Complete any required plan selection/payment and coordinate new effective and old end dates again. An income update alone does not guarantee seamless enrollment. Respond to renewal and verification notices by their deadlines.

Keep tax forms and reconcile any advance premium tax credit on the required tax return. The Connector's [current policy updates](https://betterhealthconnector.com/updates) explain removal of repayment caps beginning with tax year 2026; excess advance credits may need to be repaid in full. Ask qualified tax help about your return.

</details>

## What the coverage names mean

| Term | Meaning | Practical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer plan | Insurance provided through employment; the employer may pay part of the premium. | When does coverage end, and what contribution or replacement offer remains? |
| COBRA | Time-limited continuation of an eligible employer group plan, preserving that plan's coverage subject to its changes. | What is the full premium after the employer contribution ends, and what continuity/deductible progress remains? |
| Medicaid / MassHealth | Medicaid is a federal-state program; MassHealth administers Massachusetts Medicaid and CHIP coverage. Eligibility and benefits depend on the applicable category. | What did MassHealth determine, and is a separate plan selection required? |
| ConnectorCare | Subsidized private health plans through the Health Connector, with low/no premiums, low copays, and no deductibles under current program design. | Am I eligible, and which available insurer covers my care? |
| Ordinary Health Connector plan | Private marketplace insurance outside ConnectorCare; federal premium assistance may be available if eligible. | What is my actual premium after approved assistance, network, and cost sharing? |
| Medicare | Federal health insurance generally for eligible people 65 or older and certain younger people with disabilities or qualifying conditions. | Do separate Medicare enrollment and coordination rules apply? Start with [Medicare's own guidance](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare). |

The **Health Connector is the marketplace**, not the insurer. Medicare is different from Medicaid; some people qualify for both. Do not use this transition checklist to decide Medicare timing. The existing [COBRA guide](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#cobra-after-a-job-change) explains why losing an employer subsidy can raise the continuation bill without changing the plan.

### Network models are a separate dimension

| Model | Typical network rules | Verify in the exact plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HMO — Health Maintenance Organization | Generally network care only, with emergency exceptions. | Service area, primary-care selection, specialist referrals, and authorized exceptions. |
| PPO — Preferred Provider Organization | Usually permits out-of-network care at higher cost and specialist visits without a referral. | Separate deductible/limits, possible balance bills, and prior authorization. |
| EPO — Exclusive Provider Organization | Generally covers network care only, with emergency exceptions. | Specialist access, referral rules, service area, and prior authorization. |

These [network models](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/plan-types/) are not eligibility programs, metal levels, or quality ratings. “Accepts this insurer” does not establish participation in its particular HMO, PPO, or EPO product.

## Income and household

Complete both income timeframes accurately. MassHealth and marketplace assistance have different financial rules; a low-income month does not determine the full-year figure or guarantee a program result.

| Application question | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Current income | Answer for the application month using the requested income type and payment frequency. Ordinary unemployment compensation is income; use the gross amount before withholding. Distinguish weekly, every two weeks, and twice monthly. MassHealth applies its own financial eligibility rules. |
| Projected annual household MAGI | Make a good-faith estimate for the entire coverage year, including income already received and reasonably expected for the remaining months. MAGI is a tax-based measure, not take-home pay or cash after ordinary bills. Use permitted adjustments and the application instructions. |

For a midyear job loss, start with wages and other countable income already received, add unemployment already received and reasonably expected, add reasonably expected future earnings/other countable income, and account for permitted adjustments. Do not simply multiply the current unemployment payment by a full year while omitting earlier wages. Do not subtract ordinary rent, debt, or spending as if all expenses were MAGI deductions.

If the next job's timing is unknown, use facts available now and reasonable expectations, document assumptions privately, and ask an assister how to enter uncertainty. Do not invent an offer or treat an optimistic job-search date as certain. **Never enter a lower annual income solely to obtain a subsidy.** Update the estimate when facts change. See the [Massachusetts income questions](https://betterhealthconnector.com/how-do-i-answer-questions-about-income) and [federal annual-income explanation](https://www.healthcare.gov/income-and-household-information/how-to-report/); the Massachusetts page still has 2025 examples, so use the actual application year.

For marketplace assistance, the household generally follows the tax filer, spouse, and tax dependents for that year, including people not seeking coverage. Sharing a home with roommates or relatives does not automatically make everyone's income one tax household. Do not omit someone solely because they have other insurance. Medicaid/MassHealth rules, non-filers, dependent children, custody, and marital situations can require different treatment. Answer the application's relationships and tax questions; ask an assister about exceptions instead of choosing the smallest household. See the [federal household guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/income-and-household-information/household-size/) and [MassHealth eligibility explanation](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/eligibility-for-health-care-benefits-for-masshealth-the-health-safety-net-and-childrens-medical-security-plan).

For expected use, add **premiums for the comparison period + expected cost sharing + anticipated noncovered costs**. Deductible payments are part of cost sharing; do not add them twice. For a high-use covered in-network scenario, compare premiums plus the applicable out-of-pocket maximum, accounting for amounts already credited. Premiums, noncovered care, and some out-of-network bills are outside that maximum. A period crossing a reset date needs separate calculations; do not assume deductible progress transfers to a new plan.

A low-premium Bronze plan can cost more with frequent care or expensive prescriptions than a higher-premium Silver/Gold option. The reverse can be true for other care patterns. Compare the actual approved assistance and plan terms; ConnectorCare has its own program cost sharing. Use the existing [total health-plan costs reference](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#health-coverage-and-medical-bills). Metal level does not establish provider access or clinical quality.

## Verify providers and prescriptions

- **Providers:** check the exact plan/year/network with both insurer and provider billing office. Verify each clinician, location, hospital/facility, laboratory, and important service; ask about accepting new patients, referrals, and authorization. An insurer directory is a starting point to confirm; a third-party scheduler or directory is not final proof.
- **Prescriptions:** privately check each exact medicine, formulation, strength, and quantity in the plan-year formulary. Confirm tier and actual cost, any separate drug deductible, prior authorization, step therapy, quantity limits, and required specialty/mail-order pharmacy. A covered drug class or another plan from the same insurer is insufficient.
- **Pharmacies and continuity:** verify the dispensing pharmacy's network and specialty rules. Ask whether existing authorizations transfer, what must be resubmitted, and whether any transition refill or continuity-of-care arrangement is available. Confirm exceptions with the insurer and prescribing team; do not assume approval or change treatment based on this checklist.
- **Evidence:** record the plan document/version, date, contact/reference number, answer, and unresolved follow-up privately. The [federal prescription guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/using-marketplace-coverage/prescription-medications/) explains formulary checks and exceptions.

## Medical, dental, and vision are separate handoffs

| Benefit | Before changing continuation |
| --- | --- |
| Medical | Confirm replacement enrollment, payment, effective date, network, medicines, and each covered person's status. |
| Dental | Check whether separate enrollment/payment is needed, dentist network, deductibles, annual benefit maximum, waiting periods, exclusions, and work already underway. An annual benefit maximum limits insurer payment; it is not an out-of-pocket maximum. See [Connector dental coverage](https://betterhealthconnector.com/learn/plan-information/dental-coverage). |
| Vision | Check routine exams, frames/lenses or contacts, allowance/frequency, and network separately from medical eye care. Ask whether an employer continuation benefit can remain if medical COBRA ends. |

For each row, record the replacement start date, requested old last covered day, administrator approval, and final bill. Do not accidentally cancel dental or vision through a blanket medical termination request, and do not assume each benefit can be continued independently without checking the plan.

## Recheck before relying on this guide

Confirm the current year's enrollment calendar, eligibility rules, premium deadline, plan documents, and agency notices. Massachusetts has announced changes spanning 2026–2027; follow [MassHealth updates](https://www.mass.gov/federal-changes-affecting-masshealth-medicaid-members) and [Connector updates](https://betterhealthconnector.com/updates). Old threshold tables, screenshots, and another member's determination cannot decide your application.
