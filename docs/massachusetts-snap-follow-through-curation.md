## Editorial Boundaries and Reuse

- The contact script, suggested supervisor request, private log, and duplicate-avoidance sequence are editorial navigation aids. They do not assert a guaranteed supervisor route, automatic extension, or agency rule forbidding a necessary new application.
- There is no sourced universal waiting interval for a late call or missing notice. The guide uses actual deadlines, worker instructions, current opening hours/closures, and prompt follow-up instead. A holiday does not supply a new deadline.
- SNAP household rules are verified against Massachusetts DTA. Complex arrangements and exceptions go back to the agency; no health-coverage tax-household rule is applied mechanically to SNAP.
- The public DTA help pages display different help-desk email domains. Link the official contact route rather than hard-coding an email address; technical support is not a proof-submission or deadline-extension channel.
- Reuse the existing DTA Connect Atlas entry and the catalog's application, verification, reporting, financial-regulation, and DTA Policy Online resources. Supporting deep links belong in the workflow; no duplicate catalog hubs, new IDs, URL migrations, Atlas applicability claims, or metadata backfills are needed.
- Counts remain **5,355 catalog resources, 36 collections, 479 topics, and 147 Atlas resources**. Existing resource entries, IDs, headings, source ownership, and locale behavior remain unchanged. The short link inside the existing collection guide reaches the full Markdown workflow; a second link serves the Massachusetts resource section and Atlas readers.
- Exclude income thresholds, exact benefit amounts, individual case facts, real identifiers, addresses, medical information, and private screenshots/documents. No app UI, search algorithm, dependency, budget, or generated output is changed.

## Medium Article Angle

**Working title:** “I Finished My SNAP Interview—Now Where Did My Case Go?”

**Reader promise:** Give Massachusetts applicants one practical next action when an interview has happened but the written request, portal case, or linking identifier is missing.

**Outline:**

1. Open with a clearly hypothetical situation: an interview is complete, but no follow-up notice is visible. Do not adapt a person's identifiable case.
2. Separate application submission, interview completion, proof review, account linking, and approval. An EBT card is not an eligibility decision.
3. Explain how the current MassHealth form can forward a SNAP application when elected and signed; confirm receipt instead of assuming either an automatic transfer or no application.
4. Give the missed-call script and the short questions about required proof, written notice, and deadline. Explain SNAP food households with the required family-grouping caveat.
5. Explain missing-ID recovery, current linking instructions, and upload confirmation versus completed review.
6. Finish with the private paper-trail checklist, notice-based deadlines, and agency/outreach help. Do not promise a wait time or approval.

Link the canonical guide and primary sources, show the source-check date, and recheck before publication. This is an angle and outline, not a published article or individual benefits advice.
