# Massachusetts unemployment appeal hearing: prepare one step at a time

**Massachusetts only · public information and private worksheets · sources checked September 23, 2026.** This guide helps you prepare for a Department of Unemployment Assistance (DUA) hearing after an appeal has been filed. It cannot decide an appeal or interpret your particular notice. Read the current **determination, appeal confirmation, Notice of Hearing, and case file** for the issues, instructions, and deadlines that apply to you. Recheck the [official DUA hearing guide](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/get-ready-for-an-unemployment-benefits-appeal-hearing) before acting. The source check is an agent observation, not a human legal review.

**One next action:** Find your Notice of Hearing. Write its date, time, hearing format, check-in instructions, and every issue being heard in a *private* note. If a deadline or exhibit is unclear, contact the DUA Hearings Department through the route on the notice or the [official hearing page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/get-ready-for-an-unemployment-benefits-appeal-hearing). If you have not yet appealed a determination, protect the appeal deadline through [DUA's claimant appeal instructions](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/appeal-an-unemployment-decision-as-a-claimant); preparing for a hearing does not file an appeal.

## The route through a hearing

1. Decode the notice and identify **each** determination and legal question.
2. Separate appeal timeliness and any good-cause question from the underlying benefit or RESEA question.
3. Read the case file, inventory exhibits, and submit new evidence through the authorized route **before** the hearing.
4. Build a one-page timeline and short, truthful answer prompts; ask witnesses or a representative for help if needed.
5. Test the hearing technology and attend as instructed. Answer the examiner's questions and correct mistakes openly.
6. Wait for the written decision, read its findings and next deadline, and preserve proof of any further filing.

A scheduled time block is a reservation, **not** a prediction of how long testimony or questioning will take. This is an administrative hearing with evidence and a review examiner, not a customer-service call. [G.L. c. 151A, § 39(b)–(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter151a/Section39) provides for testimony, a hearing record, and a decision based on the issues and evidence presented. DUA's [hearing procedure description](https://www.mass.gov/doc/the-employers-guide-to-unemployment-insurance/download) explains that the proceedings are digitally recorded and witnesses giving testimony are placed under oath. Follow the examiner's directions about notes, witnesses, and exhibits.

## 2. Separate the questions before gathering evidence

| Question | Evidence to locate | What this question does **not** settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was the request for a hearing filed within the applicable time? | Determination mailing/delivery information; appeal submission and receipt. | Whether the underlying determination was right. |
| If the appeal was late, is there a legally supported reason for the delay? | A factual account of what prevented filing, when the obstacle arose/ended, and what you did upon learning or being able to act; any authentic supporting records. | Whether a missed RESEA task had good cause, or whether the underlying benefits are owed. |
| What underlying issue is actually listed for hearing? | Relevant agency notices, career-center records, truthful weekly records, and evidence bearing on **that** requirement and period. | The late-appeal question, if one is separately listed. |

[DUA's claimant instructions](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/appeal-an-unemployment-decision-as-a-claimant) say to file within **10 days of the determination's mailing date**. [430 CMR 4.13–4.14](https://www.mass.gov/doc/430-cmr-4-benefit-series/download) allow an extension for a filing made within 30 calendar days if qualifying circumstances **beyond the party's control** prevented timely filing; the agency decides whether the facts meet the rule. [G.L. c. 151A, § 39(b)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter151a/Section39) does not allow an ordinary good-cause extension beyond 30 days. **Different, narrow exceptions** appear in 430 CMR 4.15, and 430 CMR 4.13(4) addresses certain preferred-language notice circumstances. DUA's public instructions describe approval after 30 days as possible only in very limited circumstances. Ask for case-specific help promptly; do not self-disqualify or assume an exception applies. Read the actual notice and regulation before relying on any calculated deadline.

If RESEA compliance is the underlying issue, use the separate [Massachusetts RESEA recovery guide](massachusetts-resea-recovery.md). Later completion of a task may be relevant evidence, but it does not by itself prove why an appeal was filed late, reverse earlier determinations, or guarantee back benefits. “I tried my best” is not a substitute for actual dates and circumstances. Explain what happened truthfully; the examiner applies the legal standard. If the notice lists only a timeliness issue, **do not assume the merits will also be decided at that hearing**; ask the Hearings Department what is in scope.

## 3. Inventory the case file and submit evidence

DUA says to [gather and upload supporting documents before the hearing](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/get-ready-for-an-unemployment-benefits-appeal-hearing) and contact possible witnesses early. ### Evidence-submission checklist

- [ ] Compare every listed determination, appeal, case-file document, and exhibit with the notice; mark absent or conflicting items.
- [ ] Preserve original files and readable copies privately. Match documents to the actual event and date, rather than editing a document to make a timeline look cleaner.
- [ ] Check the **current notice** for the submission route and any earlier deadline. DUA's current public guide says **View appeal → Upload appeal documentation** in the worker account; follow the current notice if its instructions differ.
- [ ] Submit new evidence before the hearing using the verified route; keep the receipt privately and check that the submission appears in the case file. If unsure whether an item was received, ask the Hearings Department.
- [ ] Contact the Hearings Department in advance about video or audio evidence; DUA says it must approve the technology used to show it.
- [ ] Tell any necessary witness the correct joining instructions and check whether an interpreter, accommodation, or representative is needed.
- [ ] Prepare to identify the relevant exhibit when answering; ask the examiner how to handle missing or newly discovered material instead of assuming it has entered the record.

## 5. Prepare for testimony without scripting it

Use a few prompt cards in a private note. For **each issue**, write only what you know:

```text
WHAT HAPPENED:
WHY IT HAPPENED:
WHAT I DID WHEN I REALIZED:
WHAT I ACTUALLY COMPLETED AFTERWARD:
WHICH EXHIBIT SUPPORTS OR DIFFERS FROM MY MEMORY:
WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN:
```

Listen to the question, answer it directly, and include relevant facts even if they are uncomfortable. Do not invent a date, omit a relevant fact strategically, embellish, backdate a record, or claim an action occurred merely because it was planned. The examiner may ask follow-ups and control use of notes and exhibits. A person can be nervous and still give accurate testimony; nervousness by itself does not resolve credibility or eligibility.

Short phrases to use **when true and appropriate**:

- “I want to answer accurately; may I look at my notes?”
- “I'm nervous. May I take a moment to gather my thoughts?”
- “I don't remember the exact date; I believe it was approximately [date].”
- “Let me correct my earlier answer so the record is accurate.”
- “Could you repeat or clarify the question?”

These are requests, not promises of a pause or permission to read a prepared speech. If a question is unclear or you cannot hear, say so. Do not independently record the hearing unless the current rules explicitly allow it.

## 6. Test the virtual hearing and attend

The [current DUA virtual-hearing guide](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/virtual-unemployment-appeal-hearings) describes Webex and a Virtual Hearing Center. Platform, links, and timing can change; your notice and current DUA instructions control. For phone or in-person hearings, use the specific instructions on the [DUA preparation page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/get-ready-for-an-unemployment-benefits-appeal-hearing) and your notice.

- [ ] Read the notice early; keep its joining instructions, case-file access, and regional Hearings Department contact route handy.
- [ ] At least two days before a virtual hearing when possible, test the same device, browser/app, camera, microphone, speakers, internet, charger, and DUA's test meeting. Pick a quiet, private location.
- [ ] Have the notice, case file, submitted exhibits, one-page timeline, and concise prompts available. Close unrelated or private windows you will not need.
- [ ] About 10 minutes before a **virtual** hearing, follow the current DUA guide and your notice to enter the Virtual Hearing Center or confirmation link. Check waiting-room status and notifications. Do not substitute this virtual check-in guidance for phone or in-person instructions.
- [ ] If you cannot connect, contact the Hearings Department **as soon as possible** using the route in the notice; document the attempt privately. Do not assume silence or a connection failure postpones the hearing.
- [ ] When called, follow the examiner's instructions, answer truthfully, ask for a pause or clarification if needed, and remain available until dismissed.

## 7. After the hearing: wait for the written decision

1. Make a short, private factual note of the date, issues addressed, documents discussed, any follow-up instruction, and any technical interruption. Do **not** treat the examiner's tone, hearing length, or a portal label as an outcome.
2. Monitor the delivery route specified by DUA. The [DUA preparation page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/get-ready-for-an-unemployment-benefits-appeal-hearing) says updates come by mail or email based on preferences and advises calling the Hearings Department if no decision arrives after 30 days; that is a follow-up point, **not** a guaranteed decision date.
3. Read the written findings, result, affected issues and weeks, and any review rights and deadlines. Keep the decision privately. If favorable, verify the claim and payment history with DUA; do not assume immediate or automatic retroactive payment. If unfavorable, consider the actual Board of Review instructions and [legal help](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#taxes-and-legal-aid) promptly. A denial alone is not a finding of fraud, nor does it prove that nothing else on the claim could be affected.
4. [The Board of Review's current instructions](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/file-an-appeal-with-the-board-of-review) say an appeal from a hearing decision must be filed within **30 calendar days from the date on the Hearing Appeal Results page**. Verify the actual decision and current filing route; preserve proof of any filing. If benefits remain in dispute, follow DUA's instructions for truthful weekly requests while review is pending. A Board appeal and any later court route are separate steps, each with its own rules.
