## Editorial Boundaries and Maintenance

- The roughly two-week preparation window is editorial, not an agency deadline. It must never obscure an earlier TOP deadline, notice response, or appeal date.
- The four support states organize private planning; they are not imported agency codes. A written award with a future payment date is distinct from cash available now. Restricted benefits, provider payments, services, and deferrals are not counted as unrestricted income.
- Balance divided by weekly rate is an estimate only. Do not publish an exhaustion calculator without handling partial weeks, adjustments, disputed weeks, benefit-year limits, and agency confirmation.
- Hardship questions request verified terms; they do not assert automatic protection from collection, shutoff, eviction, fees, or interest. Pending aid never pauses a deadline by itself in this workflow.
- No changes are made to resource entries, metadata, topics, count ledgers, portal code, or payload budgets. Expected totals remain **5,355 resources, 36 collections, 479 topics, and 147 Atlas resources**; Financial Relief remains **58 resources**.
- At refresh, update the dated EB snapshot and source row together, inspect the current TOP form/overview/statute, and preserve the distinction between new evidence and unresolved individual questions. Existing support guides retain their own source notes.

**Title:** “Your Unemployment Is Ending Soon: Treat It as a Transition Window, Not a Mystery Cliff.”

**Reader promise:** Identify the real endpoint, check current options, and make an essential-expense plan while decisions are pending.

**Outline:**

1. Begin with a fictional person seeing a smaller remaining balance. Avoid private amounts, employers, diagnoses, debts, or copied portal screens.
2. Separate exhausted benefit credit, benefit-year end, a payment hold, and a missed weekly claim.
3. Show how to verify the last payable week and distinguish it from the deposit date.
4. Explain the dated EB check and conditional TOP/RED branch, including the paid-week deadline and legitimate late-application inquiry.
5. Walk through food, housing/utilities, coverage/medication, appropriate cash/disability screening, and realistic job-income timing.
6. Use the four support states and a small bill/cash plan to keep pending applications from becoming imaginary spending money.
7. End with the two transition checklists, private evidence routine, and maintained guide.
