## Editorial Boundaries and Reuse

- Rebilling, retroactive authorization dates, discount transactions, network exceptions, allowed amounts, accumulator credit, and pending-PA filing are questions for the actual plan. The guide does not invent a universal right to any of them.
- The evidence checklist, call script, sequencing, and private worksheet are editorial aids. They do not replace a claim form, written appeal, or clinician's medical explanation. Current medication access has a separate prescriber/pharmacist route; no treatment change or cash purchase is recommended.
- Reuse existing prescription assistance, HealthCare.gov appeals, Medicare appeals, SHIP, and EBSA catalog entries. Supporting form and procedural deep links stay in the guide; no duplicate catalog resources, IDs, Atlas applicability, or metadata backfills are introduced.
- Counts remain **5,355 catalog resources, 36 collections, 479 topics, and 147 Atlas resources**. The Financial Relief list remains **58 resources**. A short link inside the existing portal guide and one in its prescription section reach the full workflow without adding a portal topic.
- No private case facts, receipts, diagnoses, member identifiers, claim numbers, or screenshots are included. Keep completed worksheets outside public repositories and publishing systems.

## Medium Article Angle

**Working title:** “Paid for a Prescription While Insurance Waited? Keep These Records.”

**Reader promise:** Help a reader choose the next administrative step and preserve the evidence for a possible refund, without promising one.

**Outline:**

1. Open with an explicitly hypothetical cash purchase during PA review. Separate today's medication-access problem from reimbursement for that past fill.
2. Explain the four main situations: PA pending, later approved, denied, or a discount-card purchase. Approval dates and transaction routes matter.
3. Show the receipt and PA chronology checklist. Explain why a register slip may be insufficient.
4. Ask about pharmacy rebilling, then the plan's direct member reimbursement form, rules, and deadline. Preserve timely filing while PA remains pending.
5. Read the payment explanation and use the appropriate written appeal and assistance route. Separate commercial, employer-plan, Part D, and Medicaid processes.
6. End with the blank private worksheet and links to current official instructions.

Do not publish refund amounts, a universal deadline, a guaranteed-success headline, or identifiable anecdotes. Keep sources and their check date near the relevant claims. Link the [canonical guide](prescription-reimbursement.md) as the maintained destination.
