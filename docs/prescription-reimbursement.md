# Prescription Reimbursement After a Prior-Authorization Delay

**United States · navigation and recordkeeping · sources checked September 18, 2026.** If you paid for a prescription while prior authorization (PA) was pending, start with the receipt and the plan that covered you on the fill date. Ask whether the pharmacy can rebill or you must file a **direct member reimbursement (DMR)** or **prescription reimbursement claim**. Reimbursement is not guaranteed; approval of a PA does not by itself settle payment for an earlier purchase.

**If medication access is at risk now, contact your prescriber and pharmacist promptly.** Ask the prescriber about the plan's urgent coverage-review process when medically appropriate. Recovering money for a past fill and securing the next fill are separate tasks. Do not stop, ration, switch, or change a prescribed treatment based on this guide, or assume you must pay cash to preserve an appeal.

Return to [prescription assistance](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#prescriptions), [health coverage and medical bills](../lists/financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#health-coverage-and-medical-bills), or [health-plan appeals and counseling](../lists/legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-health-care-and-education-rights).

## Start With the Transaction You Actually Made

| Situation | Next action | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid cash while PA is pending | Save the itemized pharmacy receipt; ask the plan how to file on time while authorization is unresolved. | Whether the drug and original fill will qualify for coverage and reimbursement. |
| PA was later approved | Obtain the approval notice and effective dates; ask whether they cover this drug, strength, quantity, and original fill date. Then ask about pharmacy rebilling. | A future approval date may leave the earlier purchase unresolved; ask about review of that date or an exception. |
| PA was denied | Request the written coverage decision and appeal instructions; ask how the payment claim and coverage appeal should be linked. | A PA appeal and a reimbursement claim may have separate references, evidence, and deadlines. |
| Used a discount card or coupon instead of insurance | Tell the pharmacy and plan exactly how the purchase was processed; retain the discounted receipt. | Discount cards are not insurance. Ask whether that transaction can be rebilled or submitted, and whether any amount counts toward plan spending requirements. |
| Insurance processed the fill, but you paid a deductible, copay, or coinsurance | Ask for the claim explanation and calculation; challenge an error through the plan's correction or appeal route. | An amount you paid yourself is not automatically a reimbursable cash claim. |

These are questions to take to your plan, not a universal eligibility test. [Express Scripts' member FAQ](https://www.express-scripts.com/frequently-asked-questions) makes reimbursement conditional on the member's plan. [Optum Rx's forms hub](https://www.optumrx.com/en/forms.html) separates general, Medicare Part D, and state Medicaid forms; use only the form confirmed for your coverage.

## 1. Preserve Evidence While PA Is Pending

Keep this checklist in a private folder. Collect what exists now; ask the pharmacy or plan for missing details instead of inventing them.

- [ ] Itemized pharmacy receipt for each fill, showing the fill date, drug name and strength, quantity, days' supply, and amount actually paid. Keep payment proof too; a card statement or register slip alone may not contain the required prescription details.
- [ ] Prescription/Rx number, National Drug Code (NDC) when available, pharmacy name/address, and prescriber details. Ask for a replacement prescription receipt if needed.
- [ ] Whether insurance, a discount card, another insurer, or cash was used; any claim rejection, explanation of benefits (EOB), refund, or payment already received.
- [ ] Plan and pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) covering the fill date; privately retain the identification details the actual form requests.
- [ ] PA submission date, reference number, missing-information requests, decision date, and approved effective dates or written denial. Ask the prescriber to confirm submission rather than assuming a request was sent.
- [ ] Insurer/PBM and pharmacy messages, letters, and a dated contact log with the representative, reference number, instructions, and promised next action.
- [ ] Prescriber documentation requested for review, including any clinically relevant effect of the delay on continuity of care. The clinician supplies the medical explanation.
- [ ] Claim and appeal deadlines, their source, and proof of each submission. Keep copies before sending any required originals.

The [Optum Rx prescription reimbursement form (PDF)](https://www.optumrx.com/content/dam/optumrx-o5/PRR%20form_OTC%20Contraceptives_508.pdf) illustrates the distinction between an itemized pharmacy receipt and a register receipt, and includes waiting for drug approval as a reason for requesting reimbursement. Its instructions are an example, not the rules for every plan.

## 2. Ask the Plan to Confirm the Rules for This Fill

Use the member-services number on your card or the official member portal. Ask who administers the prescription benefit, especially if your insurer or PBM changed after the purchase. Request the relevant Evidence of Coverage, benefit booklet, Summary Plan Description, or claim instructions and the section supporting the answer.

| Confirm | Ask directly |
| --- | --- |
| Coverage and benefit | “Was I eligible on the original fill date, and does this belong under the pharmacy or medical benefit?” |
| Authorization | “Does this PA approval cover the original date, drug, strength, and quantity? If not, what review or exception can I request?” |
| Pharmacy network | “Was this pharmacy in network for my plan on that date? If not, is an exception available and what evidence is required?” |
| Correct process | “Can the pharmacy reprocess this purchase, or should I submit a direct member reimbursement claim? Which form and destination apply?” |
| Amount | “What allowed amount, deductible, copay, coinsurance, exclusions, or other payments affect reimbursement? Could it be less than I paid, or zero?” |
| Discount transaction | “I used a discount card/coupon. Can this purchase qualify, and what would happen to deductible or out-of-pocket credit?” |
| Deadline | “What is the claim filing deadline, what date starts it, and must you receive it by then? How do I file timely while PA is pending and add the decision later?” |
| Follow-up | “What evidence is missing, how will I receive a decision, and when should I contact you again?” |

**Do not wait for the PA decision without checking the claim deadline.** Ask for written instructions for a timely submission and later supplementation. A phone inquiry, pending PA, or complaint does not establish that a claim or appeal was filed or that its deadline moved. If instructions conflict with a notice or are unavailable near a deadline, contact the appropriate [help route](#5-if-payment-is-denied-or-the-amount-is-wrong) promptly.

## 3. Ask the Pharmacy About Rebilling

With the PA decision and original receipt available, ask whether the pharmacy can submit or reprocess the **original fill** to the correct plan and refund any resulting difference. Ask about its applicable window, whether the approval covers that date, and what must happen to any discount-card transaction. Do not assume every pharmacy can do this or that a quoted refund is final before the claim processes.

[Optum Rx's Part D reimbursement FAQ (PDF)](https://www.optumrx.com/content/dam/optumrx-o5/forms/Claim-Form-Medicare-Part-D-Frequently-Asked-Questions-English.pdf) describes a pharmacy-submission option for certain recent purchases. That example is not a universal rebilling deadline. If the pharmacy cannot rebill, ask the plan about DMR without letting the filing deadline pass.

Save the corrected receipt, claim result, and any refund confirmation. Tell the plan about a reimbursement claim already submitted; ask how to amend or withdraw it if the pharmacy resolves the same expense. Report other payments accurately so the same cost is not paid twice.

## 4. Submit the Member Reimbursement Claim

Use the current form and submission channel supplied for your plan and fill date. Complete required fields and signatures, attach the requested prescription receipts and PA evidence, and explain the chronology briefly: original fill, payment route, PA submission, and decision or pending status. Follow the plan's instructions for additional insurance, required originals, authorized representatives, or pharmacy-completed fields.

Save a complete copy and delivery/upload confirmation. Record the claim number, expected decision date, and any missing-document deadline. Confirm receipt and whether the claim is complete; submission confirmation is not approval. If a response is late, contact member services with the reference and ask for the written status and escalation route.

For **Medicare Part D**, a request to be paid back for a drug already purchased follows the plan's payment coverage-determination process. [Medicare's drug-plan appeals guide](https://www.medicare.gov/providers-services/claims-appeals-complaints/appeals/drug-plans) directs members to make a standard written request. [CMS distinguishes payment requests from requests for benefits](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/appeals-grievances/prescription-drug/coverage-determinations); do not treat retrospective reimbursement as an expedited medication-access request.

## 5. If Payment Is Denied or the Amount Is Wrong

First ask member services for the written explanation: denial reason, relevant plan provision, calculation, missing evidence, appeal route, and deadline. A lower payment may reflect plan cost sharing or an allowed amount, rather than an unprocessed PA. Compare the explanation with the original receipt and approval dates.

If disputing the decision, **file the written appeal through the designated channel before its deadline**. Identify the claim and disputed reason; explain the requested correction and attach the supporting receipt, PA chronology, plan provision, and relevant prescriber evidence. Request the records or criteria used in the decision when applicable. Keep a copy and submission proof. An informal correction request does not substitute for an appeal unless the plan confirms it was accepted as one. See [HealthCare.gov's internal-appeal guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/internal-appeals/).

| Coverage or problem | Where to check the next route |
| --- | --- |
| Individual or fully insured health coverage | Follow the denial notice for internal appeal and any external review. [HealthCare.gov's external-review guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/external-review/) links state insurance and Consumer Assistance Program help. External review eligibility depends on the denial and plan; not every payment dispute qualifies. |
| Private employer plan governed by ERISA, including self-funded coverage | Ask the administrator which rules apply; use the Summary Plan Description and denial notice. [DOL's health-benefit claim guide](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/filing-a-claim-for-your-health-benefits) explains review and EBSA assistance. Do not infer funding or jurisdiction from the insurer's logo. |
| Medicare Part D | Follow the plan's denial notice for redetermination and later independent review, using [Medicare's Part D steps](https://www.medicare.gov/providers-services/claims-appeals-complaints/appeals/drug-plans). The catalog's [SHIP counseling route](../lists/legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-health-care-and-education-rights) can help with navigation. |
| Medicaid or CHIP | Ask the plan or [state Medicaid agency](https://www.medicaid.gov/about-us/where-can-people-get-help-medicaid-chip) which claim, managed-care appeal, or fair-hearing instructions apply; use the actual notice. |
| Another public, military, or government-employee program | Use that program's benefit and appeal instructions; do not automatically apply commercial-plan or Part D rules. |

Record the deadline for **each** review level. Confirm any special agency extension rather than assuming it applies. A regulator complaint is distinct from filing the appeal. A Marketplace eligibility appeal is also different from appealing an insurer's prescription claim denial. For help with these distinctions, use the existing [legal navigation resources](../lists/legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-health-care-and-education-rights).

## Private Call Script and Tracking Template

**Call script:** “I paid for a prescription while prior authorization was pending. Please confirm the benefit and rules for the original fill date. Can the pharmacy rebill, or do I need a direct member reimbursement claim? What form, evidence, and deadline apply while PA is unresolved? Please provide the written instructions and a reference number.”

Copy this blank template into a private note, not a public issue, comment, or shared form. Submit personal details only through the verified plan, pharmacy, prescriber, or authorized advocate's required channel. Ask about language assistance, accessible formats, or representative authorization when needed.
