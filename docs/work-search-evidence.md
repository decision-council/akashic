# Weekly Work-Search Evidence and a Separate Application Tracker

**United States · private preparation templates · sources checked September 18, 2026.** Keep a dated record of what you actually did, then prepare the weekly record your unemployment agency requires. Track each company and role separately so applications can continue across weeks without becoming duplicate activities.

**Start small:** save today's actual action, date, and evidence in a private note. The templates below help organize records; they are not an approved claim form and do not determine eligibility. Never invent, backdate, inflate, or relabel an activity to meet a quota.

## Check the Rules Before Choosing What to Report

[DOL's unemployment gateway](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/unemployment-insurance) directs readers to state programs. Confirm the agency administering your claim and any interstate instructions; do not infer the rules solely from where an employer or job board is located.

Keep this rule card beside your private log. Use current agency guidance, your handbook, and any individual work-search plan or notice. Ask the agency about unclear or conflicting instructions.

| Record privately | Confirm with the agency |
| --- | --- |
| Agency/program and source URL or notice | Which program and rules apply to this claim? When did I check them? |
| Benefit-week start and end | Which dates and time zone belong to this reporting week? |
| Required activities and frequency | What types, number, distribution across days, employer contacts, or repeat-activity limits apply? |
| Individual instructions or exemption | Does an approved plan or exemption change the requirement, for which dates, and where is that confirmed? |
| Required fields and format | Must I use a particular form, online record, portal, or required set of fields? |
| Filing and review instructions | What must I certify now, submit with a claim, retain for a later request, or bring to RESEA? What deadlines apply? |
| Evidence and retention | What proof is required, how long must it be retained, and do an audit, dispute, or appeal require longer preservation? |

Formats differ: [Texas permits a personal log containing its required information](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/work-search-requirements), while [New York distinguishes its JobZone online record from permitted written records](https://dol.ny.gov/work-search-frequently-asked-questions). Use this worksheet to prepare, then transfer information into the required format. A private spreadsheet, Markdown file, or JSON export does not automatically satisfy an agency's recordkeeping rule.

## Keep Three Views of the Same Search

| View | Question answered | Keep here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity record | What happened, and when? | Actual action/date, organization or context, outcome, evidence, and an optional activity ID. |
| Weekly work-search log | What does this benefit week require me to record or report? | Truthful activities for that week, mapped to the agency's categories and required fields, plus the submitted or retained snapshot. |
| Application tracker | Where does this company/role stand now? | Discovery, application, materials, contacts, interview history, current status, next action, and outcome. |

A counselor appointment can exist without an application. One application can have separate submission, interview, and follow-up events over several weeks. A pending status by itself is not a new activity. Keep old weekly snapshots even when the application tracker changes.

## Capture the Actual Activity

Record the action as it happens when possible. Use the action date and, if entered later, a separate **recorded-on** date. If reconstructing a missing entry from real evidence, label it as reconstructed, preserve the evidence, and leave uncertain facts unresolved. Never make it look contemporaneous when it was not.

| What actually happened | What to capture | What not to infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submitted an application | Employer, role/posting, submission date and method, materials used, confirmation or sent record. | Saving a vacancy or drafting an application means it was submitted. |
| Scouted or reviewed openings | Date, platform/employer source, search focus, saved locators, and tracked count if known. | Each result viewed is an application or separately qualifying activity. |
| Contacted a recruiter or network connection | Actual contact date/method, organization or professional context, purpose, response, and necessary evidence. | Drafting a message means it was sent, or every informal conversation qualifies. |
| Met a counselor or attended a career-center service | Provider, service/session, date, attendance evidence, and resulting next step. | Booking, registering, attending, and completing an assigned requirement are interchangeable. |
| Revised résumé, LinkedIn profile, or portfolio | What changed, date, and the relevant version or revision evidence. | Every revision is independently qualifying work search. |
| Prepared for or attended an interview | Actual preparation or interview action, role, date, and evidence. | Preparation equals an interview, or either always qualifies under every program. |

These are recording categories, not approved activities. Texas and New York publish examples including applications, workforce services, and some preparation or networking; consult the applicable rules for the exact activity. Do not assume browsing, portfolio work, training, or résumé edits count for your claim.

### High-volume scouting and later culling

Record a real scouting session as a session. Save the query/context and useful posting locators; include an approximate count only if you actually tracked a basis for it, mark it approximate, and do not turn it into a claim count. “Reviewed openings and saved a shortlist” is better than an invented total.

When you later remove unsuitable prospects, keep a short reason in the application tracker. When you actually apply, add a new submission event on that date. Do not change the earlier scouting entry into an application, or duplicate one posting because it appeared on multiple boards. Distinct applications to different roles may be separate events; how they count for benefits still depends on agency rules. Agency referrals, offers, and suitable-work obligations need the agency's instructions; privately culling a prospect does not settle those obligations.

## Weekly Work-Search Worksheet

Copy into a **private** note. Add all fields your agency requires; for example, the [Texas log (PDF)](https://www.twc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/ui/docs/work-search-log-twc.pdf) requests job type, employer/service details, contact information, and results. Supply claimant identifiers only where the official form requires them, through its verified channel.

```text
Agency/program:
Benefit week: [start] through [end]; agency time zone:
Rules/notice checked: [source, version/date, date checked]
Required activities/frequency and any confirmed exemption:
Required record format and submission/retention instructions:
Certification due / log requested due / other notice deadline:
```

| Activity ID | Actual date / recorded on | Truthful action and job type | Employer, provider, or search context | Required locator/contact/method | Result | Evidence path | Agency category and rule checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [optional ID] | [dates] | [what happened] | [organization/context] | [required details only] | [actual result] | [private location] | [verified rule or unresolved] |

Keep unresolved qualification questions in your working notes and ask the agency how to report them; do not silently mark them as qualifying. Preserve all actual activity records even if only a permitted subset is entered in the required weekly form. Do not omit a required disclosure because it is absent from that subset.

```text
Questions resolved / agency instructions and reference:
Prepared record version and private location:
Certification submitted on / confirmation / exact snapshot:
Work-search log submitted on, or retained pending request:
Missing evidence or correction needed / next action / due date:
Retention rule source / review date / unresolved audit or appeal:
```

## Separate Company and Application Audit

Use one record per company **and role/posting**, not one row for every employer forever. Add a stable private application ID if it helps link events and evidence. Keep activity history alongside current status so a later update does not overwrite the original facts.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `application_id`, `company`, `role`, `posting_reference` | Distinguish roles and duplicate listings. |
| `source_url`, `source_platform`, `discovered_on` | Where and when the prospect was found; strip tracking or access tokens. |
| `applied_on`, `application_method` | Actual submission date/method; leave null or blank until submitted. |
| `status`, `status_as_of` | For example: discovered, shortlisted, preparing, submitted, interviewing, offer, rejected, withdrawn, closed, or not pursuing. A status update is not an activity by itself. |
| `material_variant`, `evidence_paths` | Submitted résumé/cover-letter version and private supporting files. |
| `contact_reference`, `interview_stages` | Necessary professional contact locator and dated stage history; leave absent details blank. |
| `next_action`, `follow_up_on`, `outcome`, `notes` | A real next step, planned follow-up date, terminal result if any, and concise context. A planned date is not a completed action. |
| `activity_ids` | Links to actual events; non-application activities can have an empty application-ID list. |

## Map Events Into the Correct Week

1. Select actual dated events from your activity history, including application events. Use the agency's benefit-week boundaries, not the tracker's current status or the day you entered a note.
2. Check the applicable rule or individualized plan for each activity and any repeat, day-distribution, or employer-contact limits. Required details and evidence must be present; unresolved entries need agency clarification.
3. Transfer truthful facts into the official fields. Keep the activity ID privately if useful for tracing the entry back to its evidence. Do not turn generated text or a planned task into evidence.
4. Reconcile duplicates by the underlying event, not by the number of screenshots, emails, board URLs, or tracker rows. An application and its confirmation normally describe the same submission; a later real interview or follow-up is a different event whose qualification must be checked.
5. Save the exact weekly version. Later corrections should preserve the original plus a dated explanation and follow the agency's correction route if already submitted.

In the synthetic example, the September 8 submission stays on September 8. The September 14 follow-up is a separate event; carrying APP-001 into another week does not create another application. A counselor session has its own event without a company/role record. None of these facts establishes that a weekly requirement was met.

## Five-Step Routine for a Low-Bandwidth Week

- [ ] **Capture:** after each meaningful action, save its date, truthful description, and evidence. A short note is enough to start; finish required fields before reporting.
- [ ] **Log:** at week end, check the applicable rules and prepare the required weekly record from real events. This does not reduce your agency's activity requirements.
- [ ] **Reconcile:** match actual submissions to application records; distinguish drafts, scouting, counselor contacts, and later follow-ups.
- [ ] **Submit:** answer certification questions truthfully and use the required channel when the log is due or requested. A completed private log is not a submitted claim. Other reporting duties, such as work, earnings, availability, or offers, still apply.
- [ ] **Preserve:** save the submitted/retained snapshot and confirmation, then carry only active applications and future reminders forward. Keep closed outcomes in the history.

If a requirement was missed, evidence is missing, or you are unsure how to answer, contact the agency promptly about the correct reporting or correction process. Do not manufacture an activity, hide the gap, or assume asking a question extends a deadline. Ask for language, accessibility, or counselor assistance when needed.

## Evidence, Retention, and Privacy

- [ ] Save application confirmations or sent records, a useful posting snapshot or safe locator, relevant professional correspondence, attendance/completion confirmations, and dated material revisions.
- [ ] Link each item to the actual event. A job-posting URL alone may not show that you applied, and postings can disappear.
- [ ] Preserve the agency instructions used, weekly record, certification answers or available summary, submission receipt, and any later correction or request.
- [ ] Keep a private backup and check that files can be opened. Do not use public repository attachments, public share links, or an open application dashboard as your evidence store.
- [ ] Record the agency's retention rule and any later audit/appeal instructions. Do not delete records merely because an employer rejects you or a benefit payment arrives.

Retention varies: [Texas](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-benefits/work-search-requirements) specifies the benefit year or time receiving benefits, whichever is longer; [New York](https://dol.ny.gov/work-search-frequently-asked-questions) specifies one year for paper records. These are jurisdictional examples, not a nationwide deletion schedule. Verify the applicable rule and preserve records needed for unresolved proceedings.

Keep claim identifiers, benefit amounts, diagnoses, private correspondence, and real application histories out of public examples. Retain necessary professional contact information when the agency requires it, but avoid unrelated personal details, private social profiles, or full inbox exports. Share only the requested material through the verified agency or authorized helper's channel; keep originals privately when preparing a limited disclosure copy.
