## Read This Before Moving Value

### Know which role a resource is playing

| Category | What it can establish | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education or research | Concepts, evidence, methods, or an author's analysis. | That a product, asset, or strategy is suitable or safe. |
| Protocol or standard | A technical design, interface, or proposal and its documented status. | Adoption, consensus, correct implementation, or economic value. |
| Self-custody wallet | Software for controlling keys or signing transactions. | Recovery from lost secrets, malware, coercion, or a mistaken transfer. |
| Custodial service | An intermediary controls keys or account access under its terms. | Deposit insurance, continuous access, solvency, or protection from freezes and failure. |
| Explorer or analytics service | A provider's indexed view of public ledger or market data. | Identity behind an address, legality, ownership, finality, safety, or a way to reverse a transfer. |
| Trading venue or on-ramp | A place or protocol for exchanging assets or payment methods. | Best execution, fair value, liquidity, legality everywhere, or protection from counterparty loss. |
| Token, stablecoin, NFT, or DeFi protocol | Code, issuance records, market activity, or stated rights. | A stable price, redemption, copyright ownership, audited reserves, enforceable governance, or guaranteed yield. |
| Registry, license, or audit | A filing, permission, scope of supervision, or bounded assessment. | Government endorsement, complete due diligence, future compliance, or absence of exploitable defects. |
| Advocacy or industry material | A stakeholder's position, priorities, or proposed policy. | Neutral consensus or independently established evidence. |

### Risk vocabulary to keep visible

| Risk | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Custody | Who controls keys, recovery, withdrawals, and transaction approval? |
| Counterparty | Can the exchange, issuer, lender, bridge, payment partner, or other intermediary perform its obligations? |
| Volatility and liquidity | Can price move sharply, and can an order execute without a large spread or market impact? |
| Smart contract | What bugs, upgrade keys, governance, composability, oracle failures, token approvals, or economic attacks could matter? |
| Privacy and security | What do public transaction graphs, account verification, device compromise, phishing, address poisoning, coercion, or intermediary records expose? |
| Irreversibility | Who, if anyone, can recall a confirmed transfer after a mistake or compromise? |
| Legal and tax | How do classification, reporting, sanctions, marketing, custody, consumer protection, and permitted services vary by asset, activity, date, and jurisdiction? |

### A minimum pre-transaction check

1. Identify the asset, network, destination, custody model, counterparty, fees, and the exact transaction or signature being requested.
2. Confirm the official domain and software release through a second trusted channel; never share a seed phrase or private key with support staff.
3. Check whether the service and activity are available in your jurisdiction, then inspect the relevant regulator or registry while remembering that registration is not endorsement.
4. Understand recovery and withdrawal rules before funding an account; keep independent records needed for taxes, disputes, and incident response.
5. Verify the address and network on the signing device, review token approvals, and use a small test when the protocol and fee structure permit it.
6. Treat urgency, guaranteed returns, secret strategies, remote-access requests, impersonation, advance fees, and demands to pay taxes or unlock withdrawals as stop signals.

If money may already have been sent to a scammer, stop further payments, preserve transaction IDs and communications, contact the relevant financial providers promptly, and report through official channels. Anyone promising guaranteed recovery for an upfront fee may be running a second scam.

### Build and Evaluate dApps

Web3 is a broad label for applications involving decentralized protocols, accounts, or assets. Trace the actual system: interface hosting, wallets, RPC providers, contracts, upgrade authority, oracles, indexers, and storage can each have different operators and failure modes. Public code or on-chain settlement does not make every layer decentralized.

| Starting point | Learning route |
| --- | --- |
| Understand the architecture | Begin with [dApp Architecture and Learning](#dapp-architecture-and-learning), then the existing smart-contract and wallet introductions. Distinguish a frontend, a signer, an RPC endpoint, and deployed program state. |
| Build a first application | Use Remix or Scaffold-ETH 2 with a local EVM network; Foundry and Hardhat are already listed under [Open-Source Development and Operations](#open-source-development-and-operations). Solana and Anchor use a different account model and Rust-based program workflow. |
| Study trading mechanisms | Read [Decentralized Exchanges and Market Structure](#decentralized-exchanges-and-market-structure), then compare those designs with the EEA risk framework, the DeFi survey, and MEV research. Use local simulations or supplied test environments for practice. |
| Design identity and governance | Compare sign-in, smart-account authorization, naming, voting, and execution in [Identity, Smart Accounts, and Governance](#identity-smart-accounts-and-governance); a signature or vote can have consequences beyond logging in. |
| Choose storage or cross-chain infrastructure | Read [Decentralized Data, Storage, and Interoperability](#decentralized-data-storage-and-interoperability). Identify persistence, privacy, indexing delays, oracle updates, and cross-chain verification assumptions separately. |
| Consider other architectures | Compare ActivityPub federation, Automerge collaboration, and Local-First Software research with blockchain-based designs. The existing [decentralized networking resources](../dark-web-deep-web-and-anonymous-networks/README.md) provide further alternatives. |

### Use DEX Terms Consistently

| Term | Meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Decentralized exchange (DEX) | A system for exchanging assets through decentralized protocols; matching, routing, interfaces, settlement, and governance can still have different trust assumptions. |
| Automated market maker (AMM) | A pricing and liquidity mechanism governed by pool rules; constant-product, stable, weighted, and concentrated-liquidity designs behave differently. Not every DEX uses the same AMM model. |
| Aggregator or intent-based execution | An aggregator routes across liquidity sources; an intent expresses a desired outcome that solvers may compete to fulfill. Either can use AMMs, but neither is simply another pool type. |
| Liquidity provision | Supplying assets to a pool or position; fees do not guarantee profit or offset adverse price moves, depegging, divergence from holding the assets, or contract loss. |
| Price impact and slippage | Price impact comes from a trade's effect on available liquidity; slippage is the difference between an expected quote and execution. Fees and transaction-ordering effects also matter. |
| Maximal extractable value (MEV) | Value associated with transaction inclusion, exclusion, or ordering; evaluate execution and censorship assumptions rather than treating private routing as universal protection. |
