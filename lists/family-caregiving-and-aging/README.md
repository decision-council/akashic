## Start Here

**Urgent safety comes first.** For immediate danger or a medical emergency, contact local emergency services (911 in the United States). For elder abuse, neglect, or resident concerns, use the separate [reporting and advocacy routes](#safety-reporting-and-resident-advocacy). For child abuse concerns, use [Childhelp and crisis support](../health-and-well-being/README.md#crisis-and-immediate-support); for official reporting contacts, custody, investigations, or court papers, use [family legal help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#family-children-domestic-violence-and-sexual-harm). A support conversation is not necessarily a formal report or legal representation. Ask about confidentiality and reporting duties; use a safer device if yours may be monitored.

## Before Choosing Care

**Check the program:** ask the licensing authority whether this type of care needs a license and how to inspect its current status, inspection history, and complaints. Exemptions vary. A directory listing, license, or quality rating does not guarantee fit or an opening. Use the visit checklists to ask about supervision, staff checks and training, safety, emergencies, inclusion, meals, and communication with families.

**Check the arrangement:** request written fees, deposits, late-pickup charges, cancellation terms, schedule, and transport arrangements. Ask about disability accommodations, interpreters, and phone or in-person intake. Confirm subsidy eligibility, provider participation, family copayments, and when assistance could start before relying on it. For food, coverage, and other applications, use [Financial Relief](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#benefits-screening).

## Make One Contact

**1. Prepare:** note the child's age range, care schedule, location, access needs, and your next question. Pick one verified provider or agency; place-specific referral and licensing resources also live in [Atlas](../../atlas/README.md).

**2. Ask:** confirm the service, cost, eligibility, availability, and contact person. Request the actual document checklist and accepted alternatives; ask whether identity, income, health, or custody records are necessary and how to send them securely.

**3. Save privately:** keep the contact/date, application receipt, written terms, requested documents, and any notice deadline. Keep children's names, addresses, birth dates, school details, records, and case histories out of public issues and shared examples.

**4. Follow up:** record the next action and agreed date. Ask how to check status, update a waiting-list entry, or question a decision. Seek qualified legal help promptly for court papers or unclear authority to consent; a pending application does not resolve those questions.

## Plan Aging and Caregiver Support

**1. Prepare:** start with the older adult's priorities and preferred living arrangement, people they want involved, language, and communication needs. Ask permission before sharing information. Being a relative, providing care, or having a diagnosis does not by itself give someone authority to make another adult's decisions; use [qualified elder-law help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) for unclear consent or authority.

**2. Assess needs and preferences:** discuss meals, getting around, personal care, household tasks, companionship, and the caregiver's own rest, work, and health. Ask the care team about clinical concerns and an appropriate assessment; use [appointment preparation](../health-and-well-being/README.md#appointments-referrals-and-ongoing-care). Compare options with the person rather than assuming home or residential care is always best.

| Option to discuss             | Questions to take to the agency or provider                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Support at home               | Which tasks need help, at what times, and who covers gaps? Distinguish household/personal assistance from skilled health care. Use [Housing's adaptation routes](../housing-home-and-utilities/README.md#home-repairs-and-adaptations) for assessment and modification questions.            |
| Community and daytime support | Could meals, transport, adult day services, or respite meet the need? Check accessible pickup, attendance hours, staff capabilities, and what happens outside program hours.                                                                                                                 |
| Residential support           | What care can this setting provide as needs change? Ask the responsible licensing authority about inspection and complaint records, and ask the provider about staffing, resident choices, visiting, total fees, and discharge terms. A listing is not a recommendation or an available bed. |

**3. Contact services:** use [Eldercare Locator](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#health-and-family-programs) to find the appropriate aging agency, including state, tribal, or territorial routes. Ask about a needs assessment, caregiver education, respite, adult day services, and a backup contact. For meals, use [Meals on Wheels America](../awesome-abundance/community-sharing-and-material-resources/README.md#food-and-household-essentials); for rides, use [transport assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#transportation). Check dietary/access needs, schedules, reservations, and service area with the actual provider.

**4. Confirm cost and eligibility:** request written fees, what is included, extra charges, waiting lists, and the earliest possible start. Ask the agency or insurer which exact service is covered, whether authorization is needed, what you would pay, and how long support lasts. [Medicare's long-term-care explanation](#care-costs-and-coverage) distinguishes ongoing custodial support from skilled care; do not assume Medicare, Medicaid, or a private policy will pay. Request the necessary document list and a secure submission method before sending records.

**5. Follow up:** keep the contact/date, agreed next step, application receipt, and written cost information privately. Confirm who will call whom and when; ask about alternatives if there is a delay. Revisit the arrangement with the person when preferences, needs, or caregiver capacity change. Keep names, addresses, health records, account numbers, and case details out of public issues and shared examples.

## Safety, Reporting, and Resident Advocacy

**Immediate danger or a medical emergency:** contact local emergency services (911 in the United States). A referral site, caregiver helpline, or ombuds office does not dispatch emergency help.

**Suspected elder abuse, neglect, or exploitation:** use DOJ's “Find Help or Report Abuse” in [Legal's elder-rights section](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) to reach the state or territory Adult Protective Services reporting route. Ask the receiving agency about jurisdiction, confidentiality, and reporting duties; it can identify the appropriate authority for the setting. The DOJ page and NCEA information hub do not themselves investigate your report. Use a safer device if yours may be monitored.

**Problems in a nursing home, assisted living, or another covered care setting:** use the [Long-Term Care Ombudsman Locator](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) for resident-directed advocacy. Ask about its scope and consent requirements. An ombudsman, APS, a licensing regulator, and a personal lawyer have different roles; advocacy is not a substitute for an urgent response or any required report.

**Routine support or caregiver strain:** use [caregiver education and dementia support](#caregiver-education-respite-and-dementia) and the local aging agency. A support conversation does not necessarily file an abuse report or arrange care.

## Caregiver Education, Respite, and Dementia

Ask the local aging agency about in-home, daytime, or short-stay respite and caregiver training. Confirm the person's preferences, staff skills, dementia/access needs, costs, and backup arrangements. The existing [Alzheimer's Association Helpline and support hub](../awesome-abundance/health-well-being-and-accessibility/README.md#crisis-and-immediate-support) offers dementia-related guidance and community-support routes; it is not emergency dispatch or a clinical assessment. Caregivers can also seek support for their own health through [Health and Well-Being](../health-and-well-being/README.md#everyday-care-and-prevention).
