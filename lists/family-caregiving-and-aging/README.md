## Start Here

**Urgent safety comes first.** For immediate danger or a medical emergency, contact local emergency services (911 in the United States). For elder abuse, neglect, or resident concerns, use the separate [reporting and advocacy routes](#safety-reporting-and-resident-advocacy). For child abuse concerns, use [Childhelp and crisis support](../health-and-well-being/README.md#crisis-and-immediate-support); for official reporting contacts, custody, investigations, or court papers, use [family legal help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#family-children-domestic-violence-and-sexual-harm). A support conversation is not necessarily a formal report or legal representation. Ask about confidentiality and reporting duties; use a safer device if yours may be monitored.

## Before Choosing Care

**Check the program:** ask the licensing authority whether this type of care needs a license and how to inspect its current status, inspection history, and complaints. Exemptions vary. A directory listing, license, or quality rating does not guarantee fit or an opening. Use the visit checklists to ask about supervision, staff checks and training, safety, emergencies, inclusion, meals, and communication with families.

**Check the arrangement:** request written fees, deposits, late-pickup charges, cancellation terms, schedule, and transport arrangements. Ask about disability accommodations, interpreters, and phone or in-person intake. Confirm subsidy eligibility, provider participation, family copayments, and when assistance could start before relying on it. For food, coverage, and other applications, use [Financial Relief](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#benefits-screening).

## Make One Contact

**1. Prepare:** note the child's age range, care schedule, location, access needs, and your next question. Pick one verified provider or agency; place-specific referral and licensing resources also live in [Atlas](../../atlas/README.md).

**2. Ask:** confirm the service, cost, eligibility, availability, and contact person. Request the actual document checklist and accepted alternatives; ask whether identity, income, health, or custody records are necessary and how to send them securely.

**3. Save privately:** keep the contact/date, application receipt, written terms, requested documents, and any notice deadline. Keep children's names, addresses, birth dates, school details, records, and case histories out of public issues and shared examples.

**4. Follow up:** record the next action and agreed date. Ask how to check status, update a waiting-list entry, or question a decision. Seek qualified legal help promptly for court papers or unclear authority to consent; a pending application does not resolve those questions.

## Plan Aging and Caregiver Support

For serious-illness planning or practical tasks after a death, use the [end-of-life path](#end-of-life-planning-and-after-a-death).

**1. Prepare:** start with the older adult's priorities and preferred living arrangement, people they want involved, language, and communication needs. Ask permission before sharing information. Being a relative, providing care, or having a diagnosis does not by itself give someone authority to make another adult's decisions; use [qualified elder-law help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) for unclear consent or authority.

**2. Assess needs and preferences:** discuss meals, getting around, personal care, household tasks, companionship, and the caregiver's own rest, work, and health. Ask the care team about clinical concerns and an appropriate assessment; use [appointment preparation](../health-and-well-being/README.md#appointments-referrals-and-ongoing-care). Compare options with the person rather than assuming home or residential care is always best.

| Option to discuss             | Questions to take to the agency or provider                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Support at home               | Which tasks need help, at what times, and who covers gaps? Distinguish household/personal assistance from skilled health care. Use [Housing's adaptation routes](../housing-home-and-utilities/README.md#home-repairs-and-adaptations) for assessment and modification questions.            |
| Community and daytime support | Could meals, transport, adult day services, or respite meet the need? Check accessible pickup, attendance hours, staff capabilities, and what happens outside program hours.                                                                                                                 |
| Residential support           | What care can this setting provide as needs change? Ask the responsible licensing authority about inspection and complaint records, and ask the provider about staffing, resident choices, visiting, total fees, and discharge terms. A listing is not a recommendation or an available bed. |

**3. Contact services:** use [Eldercare Locator](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#health-and-family-programs) to find the appropriate aging agency, including state, tribal, or territorial routes. Ask about a needs assessment, caregiver education, respite, adult day services, and a backup contact. For meals, use [Meals on Wheels America](../awesome-abundance/community-sharing-and-material-resources/README.md#food-and-household-essentials); for rides, use [transport assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#transportation). Check dietary/access needs, schedules, reservations, and service area with the actual provider.

**4. Confirm cost and eligibility:** request written fees, what is included, extra charges, waiting lists, and the earliest possible start. Ask the agency or insurer which exact service is covered, whether authorization is needed, what you would pay, and how long support lasts. [Medicare's long-term-care explanation](#care-costs-and-coverage) distinguishes ongoing custodial support from skilled care; do not assume Medicare, Medicaid, or a private policy will pay. Request the necessary document list and a secure submission method before sending records.

**5. Follow up:** keep the contact/date, agreed next step, application receipt, and written cost information privately. Confirm who will call whom and when; ask about alternatives if there is a delay. Revisit the arrangement with the person when preferences, needs, or caregiver capacity change. Keep names, addresses, health records, account numbers, and case details out of public issues and shared examples.

## Safety, Reporting, and Resident Advocacy

**Immediate danger or a medical emergency:** contact local emergency services (911 in the United States). A referral site, caregiver helpline, or ombuds office does not dispatch emergency help.

**Suspected elder abuse, neglect, or exploitation:** use DOJ's “Find Help or Report Abuse” in [Legal's elder-rights section](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) to reach the state or territory Adult Protective Services reporting route. Ask the receiving agency about jurisdiction, confidentiality, and reporting duties; it can identify the appropriate authority for the setting. The DOJ page and NCEA information hub do not themselves investigate your report. Use a safer device if yours may be monitored.

**Problems in a nursing home, assisted living, or another covered care setting:** use the [Long-Term Care Ombudsman Locator](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) for resident-directed advocacy. Ask about its scope and consent requirements. An ombudsman, APS, a licensing regulator, and a personal lawyer have different roles; advocacy is not a substitute for an urgent response or any required report.

**Routine support or caregiver strain:** use [caregiver education and dementia support](#caregiver-education-respite-and-dementia) and the local aging agency. A support conversation does not necessarily file an abuse report or arrange care.

## End-of-Life Planning and After a Death

You can start with one question. These tasks may sit alongside grief, uncertainty, relief, or other feelings; there is no required way to feel. Include the people the person wants involved, and respect their culture, relationships, and spiritual or secular wishes. If helpful, ask someone to take notes or make calls with you. If no one is available, ask a care-team social worker or local support service what help they can offer.

**Choose only the stage you need:** [before](#before-a-death), [soon after](#soon-after-a-death), or [later](#later-follow-through). These labels are not legal deadlines. Ask each agency or professional what is time-sensitive and what can wait; follow actual notices. State, tribal, territorial, and local rules differ. Being a partner, relative, friend, caregiver, or named beneficiary does not automatically authorize every decision.

### Before a Death

**1. Talk about care and wishes:** [palliative care](../health-and-well-being/README.md#palliative-and-hospice-care) supports comfort and quality of life during serious illness and can accompany treatment. Hospice focuses on comfort near the end of life under specific eligibility and care arrangements. Ask the treating team about options, services, costs, care at home, caregiver breaks, and who to call after hours or when a death is expected. An advance-care conversation records values and preferences; it is not the same as enrolling in either service.

**2. Check documents and roles:** use [advance-directive education and state/territory forms](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) to prepare questions for the care team and qualified legal help. Ask how to name a willing healthcare agent/proxy, when their authority applies, and what signatures or witnesses are needed. Share completed care documents with the right people and ask how to update them. A healthcare directive, financial power of attorney, will, and clinician's medical order serve different purposes; do not assume one replaces another or gives authority after death.

**3. Leave a practical map:** note where current documents, benefit/insurance contacts, and funeral or disposition preferences are kept. Ask qualified help about estate planning and who can act. Consider [digital legacy settings](#digital-legacy-and-account-requests) for your own accounts. Keep access keys and sensitive records secure; a helper needs only the information and permission appropriate to their task.

### Soon After a Death

**1. Identify the next responsible contact:** if a hospice or care team is involved, use its agreed contact plan. If someone may still be alive or there is an emergency, contact emergency services. Ask the responsible professional or local authority who handles certification, required notifications, and arrangements. Check who may authorize funeral, burial, cremation, or other locally permitted disposition; do not infer that authority from the relationship alone.

**2. Compare arrangements at your pace where possible:** use [funeral consumer guidance](#funeral-choices-and-after-death-tasks). For providers covered by the FTC Funeral Rule, ask for prices by phone, an itemized list when visiting, and a written statement of selections and total cost before paying. Ask what is optional, what a law or cemetery requires, what permits/transport are included, and what lower-cost choices are available. Confirm local assistance before relying on it. The rule does not cover every seller or cemetery.

**3. Start one notification and record:** use [death-certificate and survivor-benefit routes](../public-services-and-support/README.md#after-a-death-certificates-and-survivor-benefits). Ask the issuing office who may order copies, current fees, and which organizations need a certified copy. Use [USA.gov's notification guide](#funeral-choices-and-after-death-tasks) to identify relevant agencies and institutions; confirm what the funeral provider has already reported. Reporting a death is separate from claiming survivor benefits. Keep the next action and any stated deadline in a [private note](#keep-a-small-private-record).

### Later Follow-Through

**1. Confirm estate and benefit responsibilities:** use [Legal's estate/debt orientation](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#older-adults-long-term-care-and-fiduciaries) and the correct court or qualified adviser to ask whether probate or another procedure is needed, who may act, and which deadlines apply. A helper, healthcare proxy, estate representative, and beneficiary can have different powers. Do not distribute assets or assume personal responsibility for debts without clarifying the applicable rules. Ask benefit agencies, insurers, pension administrators, and qualified tax help about their separate processes.

**2. Review accounts carefully:** [unclaimed-property and pension searches](../public-services-and-support/README.md#unclaimed-money-refunds-and-settlements) may help locate records; a match does not establish ownership. Use [authorized digital-account routes](#digital-legacy-and-account-requests) and ask about data preservation before deletion. Do not sign in as the person or bypass their security. Check shared-household needs and the effects of closure before canceling services.

**3. Make room for support:** [grief education and peer support](../health-and-well-being/README.md#peer-support-relationships-and-grief) can be used at any stage, including before a death; ask about audience, access, cost, and confidentiality. Support may include chosen family, community, faith or secular groups, or professional care. There is no timetable for grief and no requirement to finish paperwork before seeking support. For help with children and young people, the existing Dougy Center resource offers age-focused material.

## Keep a Small Private Record

Use paper or a private file, whichever is easier. **Synthetic example only:** “Example Pension Office — asked which person may submit a survivor claim; requested its document checklist; waiting for a reply; next step: call using the verified number on the plan statement.” This is a tracking note, not a claim, authorization, or legal document.

Record the organization, verified contact, date, your role, question, requested documents, receipt/reference, actual deadline if one is given, and next step. Ask whether copies will do, how records are returned, and how to send them securely. Keep certificates, identifying numbers, account credentials, access keys, health records, and real case histories out of public issues and shared examples. A record index can name a secure storage location without copying its contents.

## Funeral Choices and After-Death Tasks

These pages help prepare questions and find the responsible organization. Choose arrangements that fit the person's wishes and local requirements; this collection does not recommend providers. ## Digital Legacy and Account Requests

**Planning for your own accounts:** review each service's settings, choose only the data and people you intend, and store instructions or access keys securely. Google's linked Inactive Account Manager uses inactivity, which is not proof of death. Apple's Legacy Contact is a separate process with its own evidence and data limits. These are examples, not a complete list of platforms.

**After a death:** use the provider's official process for the role you actually hold. Ask about preservation, release of permitted data, memorialization, and closure separately. Google warns that closing an account prevents later content-release requests. Do not share or use the person's password, reset credentials to impersonate them, or erase devices before clarifying authority and preservation needs. Reading help pages does not authorize access.

- [Apple Legacy Contact](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631) - Official instructions for choosing a Legacy Contact for your own Apple Account, securely sharing its access key, and following the later access-request route. Setup needs an eligible account/device; documentation varies, and some data and purchases cannot be transferred. <!-- akashic-meta: {"id":"apple-legacy-contact","resourceType":"documentation","role":"reference","authority":"official","access":["free"],"geography":["global"],"language":["en"],"platform":["web"],"account":"none","license":"unknown","status":"active","volatility":"high","reviewTier":"monthly","sensitive":["privacy","legal"],"linkStatus":"ok","linkChecked":"2026-09-18"} -->
