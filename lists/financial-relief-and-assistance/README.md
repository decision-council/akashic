## Start Here

You do not need to work through this whole page. Pick one next action.

**1. Need a person to help find a starting point?** Call 211 or use the [211 and Findhelp entries](../public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance). Ask for the program serving your ZIP code and the need that cannot wait.

**2. Choose the immediate problem:** [food](#food-and-nutrition), [rent](#housing-and-rent), [utilities](#utilities-and-communications), [medical bills](#health-coverage-and-medical-bills), or [prescriptions](#prescriptions). With an eviction, court, repossession, or shutoff notice, also seek [legal help](#taxes-and-legal-aid); an aid application does not automatically pause a deadline.

**3. If you have capacity for one more step:** check [benefits](#benefits-screening) after an income change, or contact the existing provider's hardship team through the number on your bill. Ask what help is available, what evidence they need, when they will decide, and what happens while the request is pending.

**Leaving a job or reviewing a continuation notice?** Use [COBRA After a Job Change](#cobra-after-a-job-change) to separate enrollment, dependent corrections, premiums, and benefit accounts.

### Types of help

| Label             | What it means                                                              | Check before relying on it                                                     |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Direct assistance | A program may provide money, food, coverage, or pay an eligible expense.   | Eligibility, funding, approval, payment timing, and renewal.                   |
| Payment relief    | An existing creditor or provider may change the payment schedule or terms. | Interest, fees, total cost, credit reporting, duration, and written approval.  |
| Discount          | The price may fall; an amount may still be due.                            | Actual price, participating provider, insurance interaction, and expiration.   |
| Navigation        | A directory, screener, or adviser connects you with another organization.  | Who actually administers the help; a referral is not an award.                 |
| Information only  | A guide explains options and where to ask.                                 | The current agency or provider rules; the guide cannot approve an application. |

Entries can combine labels. **Free information or a free application does not mean every downstream service is free.**

### Keep the next step small and private

Keep a private note with the program, contact, notice deadline, next action, and status: **identified → contacted → applied → awaiting decision → approved or declined**. Record a renewal date only when the provider gives one. Pending help is not available cash.

Ask for the program's document checklist before collecting everything. Depending on the program, it may request proof of identity, residence, household income, a lease or bill, insurance details, employment history, or medical evidence. Ask what alternatives are accepted if something is missing. Keep application receipts and written decisions; use the notice's review or appeal route if needed.

### Scam and privacy checks

Use the agency's own application route or an independently verified provider. Reject guaranteed grants or debt erasure, gift-card or cryptocurrency demands, and paid access to government applications. Review fees before agreeing to counseling or debt management. Do not change payment arrangements based solely on a sales pitch.

Share only what the verified program requires through its designated channel. Do not post Social Security numbers, bank details, diagnoses, benefit notices, or financial documents in public issues, forms, or mutual-aid posts. Ask directories how referrals share your information. Prescription discount cards are not insurance; compare the actual pharmacy price and plan treatment before using one.

## SNAP, EBT, and OTC Benefits

**Need just one next step?** For food assistance, use the [SNAP sources and state application directory](#food-and-nutrition), or the [Massachusetts starting points](#massachusetts-snap) if you live there. For an OTC card, call the member-services number on your actual health-plan card and ask whether your current plan includes an allowance.

### Three different things

| Term                    | What it is and who decides                                                                                             | Purchases and continuing access                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| SNAP                    | A government nutrition benefit under USDA rules, administered through state agencies that decide applications.         | Eligible food at authorized retailers; household eligibility, reporting, and recertification rules apply.                                                                            |
| EBT                     | Electronic Benefits Transfer: the payment system used to deliver SNAP benefits, not a separate eligibility program.    | The benefit account behind the card determines what can be bought. SNAP deposits follow the state's schedule; check state rules for balances, unused benefits, and card replacement. |
| Health-plan OTC benefit | A plan-specific allowance for eligible enrolled members, administered by the insurer or its designated benefit vendor. | Only that plan's approved products and shopping channels; allowance, reload, rollover, and expiration rules come from the plan. It is not SNAP.                                      |

SNAP generally cannot pay for medicines, vitamins, supplements, or hygiene supplies. A health-plan OTC allowance may cover selected nonprescription items, but a retail shelf label or another member's card is not proof of your coverage. Use USDA's food-purchase guidance and your own plan's current catalog. Check state-specific food restrictions and any applicable exceptions before shopping.

### Unemployment income and SNAP screening

**Receiving unemployment does not automatically qualify or disqualify you.** Ordinary unemployment compensation is generally countable **unearned income**. It is not wages, and the earned-income deduction does not apply to it. Report the gross award, payment frequency, withholding, and recent changes; ask the agency how it treats your payments. The federal income rule is in [7 CFR 273.9(b)(2)(ii), 2025 annual edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title7-vol4/xml/CFR-2025-title7-vol4-sec273-9.xml); Massachusetts also classifies it in the [DTA financial eligibility regulation](#massachusetts-snap).

Start with the correct **SNAP household**, not just the people on a tax return or lease. People who buy and prepare meals together generally form a household; spouses and most children under 22 living with a parent must be grouped together even if they prepare meals separately. Household size changes the applicable tests and benefit calculation.

**Gross income** is non-excluded household income before allowable deductions. **Net income** is what remains after SNAP's permitted deductions, not whatever cash remains after paying every bill. The baseline federal rules usually apply both income tests. Older-adult or disability rules and categorical eligibility can change which tests apply. States using broad-based categorical eligibility can have different gross-income or asset limits; meeting one of those limits still does not guarantee a benefit or waive other eligibility rules.

**Eligibility guidance checked 2026-09-17.** USDA's linked eligibility table is labeled **October 1, 2025–September 30, 2026**, and its page says some guidance is still being updated after the 2025 law changes. Use the effective benefit year and your state's current rules, including work, student, and noncitizen requirements. Do not carry that table's amounts into October 2026 or treat an older worked example as a current threshold. A screen is an estimate; the agency decides after an application.

### Expenses that may count

Use the [USDA eligibility and deduction guidance](#food-and-nutrition). Each category has conditions; high expenses do not make all income disappear.

| Category                              | How to think about it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Standard and earned-income deductions | A standard deduction applies in the calculation. The separate earned-income deduction applies to qualifying earnings, not unemployment compensation.                                                                                                                               |
| Dependent care                        | Qualifying care needed for work, training, or education may count; ask about applicable job-search and care-related transportation rules.                                                                                                                                          |
| Medical costs                         | Qualifying unreimbursed costs for an older or disabled household member may count, subject to the applicable rules. This is not a deduction for everyone's health spending.                                                                                                        |
| Legally owed child support            | Treatment depends on state rules and proof of the obligation and payments; it is not a general deduction for all family expenses.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Shelter and utilities                 | Rent or mortgage and allowable utilities enter an excess-shelter calculation after other deductions. Only costs above half of the adjusted income enter that deduction; caps, exceptions, or a homeless shelter deduction may apply. A utility allowance may replace actual bills. |
| Ordinary bills and debt               | Credit-card payments, car-loan payments, subscriptions, and ordinary grocery spending are not standalone SNAP deductions. Ask about a qualifying underlying medical or dependent-care expense rather than subtracting the whole debt payment.                                      |

### Gather a small screening packet

Keep this information private and use the agency's own checklist:

- State of residence; household members, ages, relationships, and who buys and prepares meals together.
- Gross wages, unemployment award/payment records, other income, payment frequency, and recent or expected changes; assets if the agency asks.
- Your share of rent or mortgage, utilities paid separately, qualifying dependent-care costs, legally owed child support paid, and eligible unreimbursed medical costs.
- Identity and residence evidence, application notices, and any information the agency requests about disability, work, student, or immigration eligibility; ask about acceptable alternatives if documents are missing.
- Application receipt, interview or verification deadline, decision notice, change-reporting instructions, and recertification date.

In Massachusetts, DTA says an initial application can be filed with your name, address if any, and signature; required verification can follow. Do not let an incomplete document folder stop you from checking the [official application instructions](#massachusetts-snap). Ask about expedited processing when food cannot wait. After approval, read your own certification notice: reporting a change, recertifying, and receiving a monthly deposit are different steps.

### Verify your actual OTC plan

An OTC allowance is not universal across Medicare, Medicaid, employer, or other health plans. Prescription coverage alone does not establish an OTC shopping allowance. The [plan resources below](#health-plan-otc-benefits) show where to verify; the insurer example describes its own plans only.

- Identify the exact insurer, plan name or ID, service area, active enrollment, and benefit year. Use this year's Evidence of Coverage or member handbook and any applicable updates, not an advertisement for a different plan or year.
- Ask whether you qualify for this allowance, whether activation is required, and which insurer-approved vendor handles it. Ask separately about any grocery or other spending benefit; it may have additional eligibility rules.
- Check the exact item or product code in the plan catalog or approved item checker, plus the participating retailer and permitted in-store, online, or mail-order channel.
- Confirm the available balance, reload schedule, unused-fund rollover, spending deadline, and what happens at year-end or when coverage ends. Never assume a monthly reload or automatic rollover.
- Before ordering, check quantity limits, shipping or other charges, returns, and how to pay any amount beyond the allowance. If a purchase is declined, contact the plan or its authorized vendor rather than assuming you have no coverage.

Use the number printed on your plan card or a vendor linked from the insurer's official site. Keep EBT and OTC card numbers, PINs, login codes, benefit balances, and health details out of public posts and issues. If another benefit application asks about support you receive, ask its administering agency how to report a plan allowance; do not infer the answer from how the card looks.

## COBRA After a Job Change

**Start with your actual notice.** Find the election or annual enrollment deadline, the administrator's verified contact, and the coverage end date. The [COBRA sources below](#cobra-continuation-coverage) explain the federal baseline; your plan documents and applicable law determine the available choices. This module's primary sources were checked **2026-09-17**. Recheck at use time, especially after a plan, household, employment, or legal change.

### Initial election and annual enrollment

COBRA temporarily continues an existing employer group health plan after a qualifying event causes coverage loss. Federal COBRA generally covers plans of private employers with at least 20 employees and state or local government plans meeting the federal requirements. Federal employee and certain church plans use different rules; smaller employers may fall under state continuation law.

**The original election** is the decision to continue coverage after the qualifying event. Each qualified beneficiary has an independent election right. **Annual COBRA open enrollment** is the opportunity to review the coming plan year's options while continuation coverage remains available. It does not restart the maximum COBRA period. Participants receive the open-enrollment rights available to similarly situated active participants; plan changes also apply to COBRA participants.

An unchanged plan can have a new price. A notice saying **“current plan is not changing”** does not establish that premiums, employer contributions, or your required action are unchanged. Read the rate sheet and instructions separately. If the notice requires active re-election, submit it by its deadline. If it explicitly continues coverage automatically, verify the default plans, dependents, and price; act if you need an allowed change. If unclear, get written administrator instructions before the deadline. Never assume every plan renews automatically or every plan requires a new election.

### Review medical, dental, and vision separately

For each benefit, record its own plan name, covered people, continuation end date, monthly premium, and next-year effective date. Ask whether benefits are separately electable or bundled under your plan.

| Benefit | Verify before confirming                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Medical | Exact network and current providers; prescriptions and formulary; referrals or authorizations; deductible and out-of-pocket amounts already credited; next plan year's changes. |
| Dental  | Separate carrier or administrator, participating dentist, annual maximum, covered treatment, and any waiting or treatment-in-progress rules.                                    |
| Vision  | Separate carrier or administrator, participating provider, exam and eyewear allowances, frequency limits, and any pending order.                                                |

**Workflow:** qualifying event → compare options and elect on time → verify active plans and dependents → read annual notice → review benefits and premiums → correct records → re-elect or verify automatic continuation → save confirmation and confirm payment/effective date. Afterward, keep payments current and revisit at the next annual notice or when coverage, employment, household, or costs change.

### Correct dependent records before final enrollment

1. Compare the election notice, current coverage, and annual enrollment screen. Distinguish a misspelled name or stale relationship label from a request to add coverage, remove coverage, or report an eligibility event.
2. Contact the administrator through the notice's verified channel before final submission when the record is wrong. Ask who maintains eligibility records, who bills COBRA, and whether a separate carrier update is needed. Updating a profile is not necessarily formal notice of a qualifying event.
3. Ask for the required evidence, accepted alternatives, secure delivery method, and deadline. Depending on the event and plan, this may include a divorce decree, marriage or birth certificate, adoption document, or other proof of the eligibility change. Send only what is required and retain delivery evidence privately.
4. Request written confirmation of the correction, coverage tier, premium, affected person's rights, and effective date. New dependents may be added through applicable open or special enrollment procedures; ask about the permitted window and required election. A newly listed dependent does not automatically acquire every independent COBRA right; children born to or placed for adoption with the covered employee during COBRA can be qualified beneficiaries.
5. Recheck each medical, dental, and vision election and the final confirmation. **An unanswered support request does not extend a deadline.** If correction is pending, ask immediately how to make a timely valid election and payment while it is resolved; escalate if needed rather than silently letting the window close.

**Generalized divorce example:** an annual screen still lists a former spouse. That display alone does not establish current eligibility or authorize deleting coverage. Ask the administrator to distinguish a stale record from separate continuation coverage and to explain the notice, documentation, election, and billing steps for everyone affected. A former spouse may have independent COBRA rights, including a possible extension after a second qualifying event. Do not infer health-plan eligibility from a divorce agreement or retirement-plan QDRO; have the actual documents and current law checked by the administrator and qualified counsel when needed.

Keep names, birth dates, Social Security numbers, case numbers, employer identifiers, medical details, and private portal screenshots out of public posts and issues. For unresolved private-sector plan questions, use the existing [DOL Ask EBSA entry](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#work-wages-benefits-and-discrimination); the CMS resource below addresses state and local government plans. Disputes may also need [qualified legal help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#find-free-and-low-cost-legal-help).

### Premium and payment checkpoint

COBRA generally permits charging up to **102% of the total plan cost**, including the former employer's share, rather than 102% of the old paycheck deduction. An employer may subsidize coverage temporarily; confirm the subsidy end date and the amount you will owe afterward. A qualifying disability extension can allow up to 150% during the additional 11 months when the disabled beneficiary continues coverage. Use DOL's guide for the conditions.

- Get the rate for each elected benefit and coverage tier, the employer contribution if any, the total monthly amount due, and the date each change begins.
- Confirm any retroactive premiums due with the initial payment, the monthly due date, payment recipient, grace period, and accepted payment method. A first payment may cover several months.
- Verify automatic payment enrollment, amount, and funding account after a rate change. A saved payment method, scheduled payment, submitted election, or bank debit alone does not confirm that the correct coverage period was credited.
- Save the election receipt, plan/dependent summary, written correction, payment receipt, and coverage dates privately. Check the administrator's paid-through date and the carrier's active coverage for each plan; resolve a mismatch promptly.
- Keep paying on the confirmed schedule while an administrative question is pending unless the administrator gives a valid different arrangement. Plans need not send monthly bills. Coverage can be suspended during a grace period and reinstated after timely payment; ask about claims while payment is processing.

### Federal deadlines and state overlays

These are ordinary federal COBRA rules, not a personalized deadline calculation. Use the DOL worker guide and your notice together; ask about applicable extensions, special plan rules, and any conflicting dates immediately.

| Step                 | Federal baseline to verify                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Initial election     | At least 60 days from the later of coverage loss or provision of the election notice.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Initial premium      | At least 45 days after the COBRA election; confirm the full amount and periods owed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Later premiums       | Monthly payment must be available, with at least a 30-day grace period for subsequent payments.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Family-event notice  | For divorce, legal separation, or loss of dependent status, follow the plan's notice procedure. The deadline cannot be less than 60 days from the latest of the event, resulting coverage loss, or being informed of the notice responsibility/procedure. This is separate from the election deadline. |
| Maximum continuation | Generally 18 months after job loss or reduced hours; up to 29 with a qualifying disability extension; up to 36 for specified spouse/dependent events or qualifying second events. Conditions and timely notices apply; coverage can end earlier.                                                       |
| Annual enrollment    | The plan's stated window governs. Annual enrollment does not reset the continuation clock or replace event-notice obligations.                                                                                                                                                                         |

For employer-reported events, the usual notification sequence is 30 days for the employer to notify the plan and 14 days for the administrator to issue the election notice; an employer that is also the administrator generally has 44 days. Starting points and special multiemployer-plan rules matter. Contact the administrator if the notice is missing. Continuation also depends on a group plan remaining available; it is not a promise of coverage after the employer ends all group health plans.

**State overlay:** identify the employer/plan type, where the policy is issued, and whether it is insured or self-funded. Ask the relevant regulator which continuation law applies, which benefits it covers, and whether eligibility, notices, premiums, or duration differ. Use the existing [NAIC state insurance department directory](../business-and-entrepreneurship/README.md#contracts-insurance-and-legal-help). The New York DFS entry below is a **New York-only example**, including state continuation of up to 36 months subject to its conditions; do not transplant it to another state or assume it applies to every self-funded plan.

### Compare COBRA with Marketplace coverage

Neither route is automatically best. Compare the exact COBRA option with an ACA Marketplace or state-exchange plan for the same people and coverage dates. Start with [HealthCare.gov's existing Marketplace entry](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#health-and-family-programs), the COBRA switching guide below, and the [total-cost reference](#health-coverage-and-medical-bills).

| Compare                   | Questions for both options                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Premium and assistance    | What will you actually pay after any employer contribution or confirmed Marketplace savings? What changes if household income or the subsidy changes?                                                    |
| Providers and continuity  | Are the specific doctors, facilities, pharmacies, and ongoing services in network for this exact plan? Confirm with the plan and providers; ask about existing authorizations and treatment transitions. |
| Deductible and total cost | What premiums, deductible, copays, coinsurance, out-of-pocket limit, and noncovered costs apply over the same period? Which amounts already paid will be credited, and when does the deductible reset?   |
| Medicines and benefits    | Are the drugs, doses, services, dental, and vision benefits included, separately purchased, or excluded? What approval or network restrictions apply?                                                    |
| Timing                    | Is enrollment currently allowed, what is the confirmed start date, and when will the old coverage end? Avoid an unintended gap or overlapping premium obligation.                                        |

Losing job-based coverage generally opens a Marketplace special enrollment window; HealthCare.gov says to enroll within 60 days after that loss. COBRA exhaustion or the end of an employer contribution can also allow a switch outside Marketplace open enrollment. **Voluntarily dropping COBRA or choosing not to pay generally does not create that right.** A routine rate increase is not necessarily the end of an employer contribution. Verify the qualifying reason, window, and effective date with the exchange before canceling. Marketplace open enrollment is a separate calendar from the former employer's annual enrollment; check the current federal or state-exchange dates. Do not wait for the COBRA election window to finish before comparing alternatives.

### Benefit accounts have different rules

Use the [IRS references below](#post-employment-benefit-accounts) and contact each account's administrator. One benefits portal can show several programs with different end dates and rules.

| Benefit                           | What to verify after employment ends                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Health FSA                        | Some health FSAs have COBRA continuation rights, with special eligibility, premium, and carryover rules. Ask whether continuation is offered, which expense dates qualify, and the claim-submission deadline. Do not equate a claim run-out period with permission to incur new expenses or assume a new annual salary-reduction election is available. |
| Dependent-care FSA                | This is not health coverage continued by medical COBRA. Ask about eligible expense dates, any plan-permitted spend-down, and the deadline to submit claims.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| HSA                               | The account stays yours after leaving a job. Continued contributions require current HSA eligibility; retaining a balance does not establish it. IRS rules permit qualified COBRA premiums to be paid from an HSA, subject to the applicable conditions and records. Verify fees and account access separately.                                         |
| Commuter or parking program       | These employment-related fringe benefits are separate from health COBRA. Confirm when payroll elections stop, which prior expenses remain reimbursable, claim deadlines, and treatment of unused balances. Do not assume cash refunds or transfer to a health account.                                                                                  |
| HRA, life, or disability benefits | Obtain each plan's separate continuation, conversion, or termination terms. An HRA may have health-plan continuation obligations; life/disability-only coverage is not covered by health COBRA.                                                                                                                                                         |

## Student Loans

These resources concern **federal** student loans. Private loans require contacting the lender about its own terms.
