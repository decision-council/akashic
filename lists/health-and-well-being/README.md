Do not start, stop, combine, or change medicines, supplements, peptides, or other treatments based on this list. Supplements and compounded drugs are not FDA-approved before marketing, and many research peptides lack adequate human safety data. ### Prepare → visit → records/referral → follow-up

**1. Prepare.** Write down your main questions and concerns, current medicines and supplements, allergies, and relevant history. Gather existing results and contact details for other clinicians. Confirm the appointment's location or connection method, access needs, likely cost, and coverage; ask about [transportation assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#transportation) if getting there is a barrier.

**2. Visit.** Explain what matters to you and any cost, access, or practical barriers. Ask the clinician to explain options, uncertainties, and next steps in plain language. Check your understanding and request written instructions, including whom to contact with questions and when the clinician wants follow-up.

**3. Records/referral.** Request the visit summary and relevant records through the provider's records office or portal. Check medicines, allergies, history, and contact details; ask how to request corrections. For a referral, confirm the receiving office has it and the needed records, then check scheduling and coverage. Ask who will communicate test results and by when.

**4. Follow-up.** Keep appointments, referral contacts, expected results, and agreed follow-up dates together. If an expected result or referral response has not arrived, contact the responsible office. For ongoing conditions, ask which clinician coordinates the overall plan and how changes will reach the rest of the team. Raise problems following the plan with that team. Send coverage or billing questions to the [financial-relief routes](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#health-coverage-and-medical-bills).

### Protect records and route concerns

Use a verified provider channel for private records; keep copies of requests and responses. Share only what is needed with the intended care team or authorized helper. Ask the provider how caregiver or proxy access works; a public resource directory should never receive your medical records.

For a care or safety concern, contact the treating team or the facility's patient-relations office and ask for its complaint process. The [patient-safety resources](#health-records-and-patient-safety) include Joint Commission's route for organizations it accredits; the state health department may offer another route. For discrimination, disability or language access, or health-information privacy concerns, use the [existing rights and HHS OCR pathways](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-health-care-and-education-rights). Insurance appeals and billing disputes have [separate routes](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#health-care-insurance-and-medical-debt); check the relevant body's scope and deadlines. Do not wait for a complaint response to seek emergency help.

**Before booking.** Reuse the [prepare → visit → records/referral → follow-up checklist](#prepare--visit--recordsreferral--follow-up). Ask whether a licensed clinician will provide the specific care you want and which options the organization offers or refers for; advertising counseling, a pregnancy test, or an ultrasound alone does not answer those questions. Confirm professional licensure, accessibility, interpreters, respectful care, and any support-person arrangements. The existing [identity-affirming care resources](#culturally-responsive-and-identity-affirming-care) and [disability and language-access paths](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#disability-and-language-access) can help with additional needs.

**Costs and practical access.** Ask the practice and plan about coverage, network participation, clinician and facility charges, tests, medicines, and follow-up costs. Ask about financial assistance before assuming a free search means free care. Reuse [health coverage and medical-bill help](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#health-coverage-and-medical-bills), [WIC and food assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#food-and-nutrition), and [transportation help](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#transportation); eligibility and available assistance vary. A clinic locator does not confirm that a particular service is covered or currently offered.

**Consent, privacy, and legal questions.** Ask how consent rules apply to your age and location, who can see portal records, what billing or insurance notices may disclose, and how the office can contact you safely. Use [Repro Legal Helpline and health-care rights resources](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#health-care-insurance-and-medical-debt) for U.S. legal questions about reproductive care; providers address clinical care, and lawyers address individual legal questions. Review a directory's requested data and privacy policy before entering information. Keep pregnancy dates, test results, intimate details, and medical records out of public issues or posts; use verified provider or legal-service channels for necessary private information.

**Routine, urgent, and emergency help.** Use scheduling and the care team's follow-up plan for routine care. [CDC's urgent maternal warning signs](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html) explain when to obtain immediate medical attention during pregnancy and in the year after delivery. Do not wait for a routine appointment, support group, or helpline callback when urgent care is needed. For a medical emergency or immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services (911 in the United States). HRSA's maternal mental-health hotline offers emotional support and referrals; U.S. suicide or mental-health crisis support is available at 988. Neither a peer group nor a legal helpline replaces emergency clinical care.

## Pregnancy and Infant Loss Support
