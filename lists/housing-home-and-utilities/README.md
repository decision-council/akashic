## Start Here

**If you can do one thing:** pick the situation below and make one verified contact. For immediate danger, use emergency services; a housing application is not an emergency response.

| Your situation                                       | First reachable step                                                                                                                                                                                           | Keep separate                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| I need somewhere safe tonight.                       | Use [211 and emergency-housing referrals](../public-services-and-support/README.md#benefits-and-emergency-assistance); call 211 where available and confirm local intake.                                      | A shelter referral does not reserve a bed; ask about household, accessibility, safety, transport, and pet needs.                     |
| I am looking for a rental.                           | Start with [housing search and counseling](#find-housing-and-counseling), then the [application and lease checks](#rental-applications-and-leases).                                                            | A listing is not verified availability; confirm the property and the person offering it before sending money or sensitive documents. |
| I need an affordable or subsidized home.             | Compare the [three program routes](#affordable-housing-programs), then contact the PHA or property.                                                                                                            | A waiting-list entry, eligibility decision, voucher, and signed lease are different stages.                                          |
| I may lose my current home.                          | Use [rent assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#housing-and-rent) and [housing legal help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#housing-eviction-and-homelessness); keep the actual notice.      | Applying for aid, disputing a bill, or contacting a counselor does not automatically stop eviction or answer court papers.           |
| I need utilities connected or have a shutoff notice. | Contact the provider through a verified bill or website; use [utility guidance](#utilities-and-shutoff-help) and [bill assistance](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#utilities-and-communications). | Starting service, arranging payment, applying for aid, and confirming a disconnection hold are separate tasks.                       |

## Prepare, Contact, Save, Follow Up

**1. Prepare:** write the next task and any notice deadline. Ask the agency, property, or utility for its actual document checklist and accepted alternatives; there is no universal packet. Share only necessary information through a verified channel.

**2. Contact or apply:** confirm who handles the task, current eligibility or screening criteria, costs, waiting-list status, and how to request language or accessibility help. Ask for phone, paper, or in-person options if the online route does not work.

**3. Save confirmation:** privately retain the application receipt, written terms, lease, notices, dated repair requests/photos, and a brief contact log. A submitted form is not an approval, payment, repair, or restored service.

**4. Follow up:** record the next action and the date the agency or provider gives you. Ask how to keep contact details current, check status, respond to missing evidence, and request review of an adverse decision. Use legal help promptly for an unclear deadline or rights dispute.

**Need a person?** Try 211, the local PHA, or a HUD-participating counselor. Ask what they can handle and what may cost money. For a dispute, use a tenant advocate or qualified legal aid; counseling is a different service. Find place-specific resources in [Atlas](../../atlas/README.md), then verify service area and intake directly. A map boundary does not establish eligibility.

**Sources checked September 17, 2026.** Recheck fees, waiting lists, forms, and rules at use time. See the [curation and source notes](../../docs/housing-navigation-curation.md) for verification limits; this date is not a human truth review.

## Rental Applications and Leases

Before paying an application charge, ask what screening is used, what fees are lawful locally, whether they are refundable, and what happens if you are declined. Review the complete lease, total move-in/recurring costs, included utilities, repair contact, renewal/termination terms, and deposit return process. Document move-in condition and keep receipts; a signed term is not necessarily enforceable.

For an inaccurate screening report or a denial based on it, use the existing [CFPB background-check guidance and legal-help routes](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#housing-eviction-and-homelessness). Keep the notice and request/report/dispute evidence privately; do not assume a successful dispute guarantees the rental.

- [Fannie Mae Renter Resources](https://yourhome.fanniemae.com/rent) - Free education from the government-sponsored housing-finance company covering rental costs, applications, leases, deposits, maintenance, and moving out. ## Repairs and Tenant Support

Report repair concerns through the property's verified maintenance route and keep dated evidence. For unresolved heat, water, leaks, pests, or other habitability concerns, ask your local housing/code-enforcement or health department and legal aid which process applies. Get local legal advice before withholding rent, deducting repair costs, or ending a lease.

For disability-related access, describe the change you need and ask about an accommodation or modification. Keep a record, share only necessary supporting information, and distinguish a rule/service adjustment from a physical alteration. The [DOJ/HUD accommodation reference and fair-housing help](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#housing-eviction-and-homelessness) explain the framework and escalation routes; coverage, responsibility for costs, and the decision are case-specific.

- [EPA Mold and Your Home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-and-your-home) - Free Environmental Protection Agency guidance on moisture, mold prevention, and related indoor-air information. Use it to understand a concern and discuss repairs; it does not determine a landlord's duties, establish habitability, or replace local inspection or medical advice. <!-- akashic-meta: {"id":"epa-mold-and-your-home","resourceType":"article","role":"education","authority":"official","access":["free"],"geography":["us"],"language":["en"],"platform":["web"],"account":"none","license":"unknown","status":"active","volatility":"medium","reviewTier":"quarterly","sensitive":["medical","legal"],"linkStatus":"ok","linkChecked":"2026-09-17"} -->

## Utilities and Shutoff Help

For a move, confirm which services the lease includes, who opens each account, start/stop dates, deposits, and the first bill. For arrears or a shutoff notice, ask the verified provider about a workable arrangement and any applicable protection; obtain confirmation of what remains due and whether disconnection is actually paused.

Use [Financial Relief](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#utilities-and-communications) for energy-bill assistance and current state disconnection contacts, and [Public Programs](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#housing-energy-and-communications) for LIHEAP, weatherization, and utility-assistance directories. State rules and provider coverage vary, including municipal utilities and cooperatives. Ask who handles complaints for your provider; do not assume an aid application creates a shutoff hold.

- [FTC Getting Utility Services](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-utility-services-why-your-credit-matters) - Free guidance on establishing gas, electric, or water service, credit checks, deposits, denial notices, payment arrangements, and budget billing.
