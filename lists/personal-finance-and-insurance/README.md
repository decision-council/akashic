## Start Here

**Choose one next step:** put the next income date and bills on a calendar; compare banking fees and deposit protection; check a credit report for errors; review borrowing costs with a nonprofit counselor; or prepare a transfer question or complaint.

Use the [private checklist and resource sections](#keep-a-private-checklist) below. If food, housing, or bills cannot wait, start with [Financial Relief](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#start-here).

## Keep a Private Checklist

**Prepare:** keep income dates, bills, statements, notices, and the question to resolve in a private note. Ask the provider which evidence is actually needed. Do not post account numbers, identity documents, passwords, or completed worksheets in Akashic, issues, or shared search links.

**Contact and follow up:** use a verified website or statement; ask about fees, phone/mail options, interpreters, and accessible formats. Save copies of submitted evidence, receipts, written terms, the response deadline, and next action privately.

**Fictional example:** “Example Bank — ask about a $5 monthly fee — called September 17 — requested written terms — follow up September 24.” These invented dates are reminders, not legal deadlines. No account data is needed here.

## Banking and Deposit Protection

Compare monthly fees and waiver conditions, minimum balances, overdraft/NSF policies, ATM and cash-deposit access, funds-availability rules, and ways to get help. Verify the actual bank or credit union, not just an app's brand. Deposit insurance protects eligible deposits against an insured institution's failure; it is not general fraud reimbursement or investment-loss insurance. Check ownership categories and limits with the insurer; see [NCUA coverage guidance](https://ncua.gov/consumers/share-insurance-coverage).

If denied an account, read the notice and use the existing [ChexSystems and Early Warning disclosure routes](../public-services-and-support/README.md#financial-foundations-and-consumer-tools). Their reports differ from nationwide credit reports; request the report used in the decision.

## Borrowing and Counseling

Before borrowing, compare APR, fees, total repayment, payment dates, variable rates, collateral, and late/default consequences; a lower monthly payment can mean a longer and more expensive loan. Use the CFPB tools above to prepare questions.

Public guides are free information. Nonprofit counselors can review a budget, but counseling and debt-management plans may carry fees. Ask about fee waivers, written terms, creditor participation, and whether a plan requires closing accounts. Confirm any promise with the creditor.

The existing [provider and commercial-service directory](../public-services-and-support/README.md#debt-finance-and-taxes) labels paid services. Commercial comparisons may earn referral revenue and omit providers. Debt settlement differs from counseling and debt management: fees, missed payments, credit damage, collection/lawsuit exposure, and possible taxes can remain. Read the [FTC debt-options guide](../financial-relief-and-assistance/README.md#debt-and-payment-hardship) before signing. No provider is ranked or endorsed here.

- [Financial Counseling Association of America](https://fcaa.org/) - Nonprofit counseling-agency network. ## Transfers and Consumer Help

Before sending, verify the recipient independently. Compare the total amount paid, exchange rate, provider/intermediary/recipient fees, amount and currency received, delivery time, and cancellation/error process. Domestic transfers and international remittances can have different protections; save the disclosure and receipt. A “zero fee” offer can still have an exchange-rate cost.

For suspected fraud, contact the payment provider promptly through a verified channel and use [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](../public-services-and-support/README.md#consumer-and-transportation-safety). For stolen identity, use [IdentityTheft.gov](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#taxes-credit-and-consumer-help). Neither route promises recovery. Never send another payment to unlock a refund.

For an unresolved financial-product problem, use the existing [CFPB complaint route](../awesome-abundance/public-programs-and-services/README.md#taxes-credit-and-consumer-help). For other products, services, or regulator routing, use [USA.gov Consumer Complaints](../legal-help-and-law/README.md#money-debt-bankruptcy-and-consumer-rights). Send necessary evidence only through the verified recipient's channel; complaint handling is distinct from legal representation.

- [CFPB Money Transfers](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/money-transfers/) - Free guidance on remittance disclosures, fees, cancellation, errors, and scams.
